# TT93 | A compact open-bridge wrist optic

TT93 is a **changed sensor fixture**, not the original 100-return benchmark. It keeps the same centered arm, clamp, opaque links, gripper, paddle and 124° wrist optics. It replaces the sight-blocking solid camera wedge with a physically opaque open rear carrier supported at the original beveled wrist shoe. The carrier uses two shaped rear struts with joined endcaps. Both PCB support ends meet the back of the board. The aperture has real open space, and the native renderer keeps the entire lens enclosure, sensor, board, screws and wrist.

The frozen aperture moves from (+6,+54,−35) to (−27,+73,−46) mm in the gripper frame, a 39.64 mm translation. The stock optic is tilted 60° relative to its old view, with a fixed installation azimuth and roll. The actual camera body is 15.00 g using the inertia of a 30×28×16 mm envelope at the measured COM. The new molded carrier, including the retained shoe, is 19.96 g with native geometry-derived COM and inertia. All masses participate in the same physical plant. The original body/fixture remains the ordinary default.

In the installed opaque scene, the optical axis and eight parallel rays around the lens's 4 mm aperture ring all pass through the actual intervening housing space. Their first collision is the table at least 1.114 m away. This is an exposed bore, not a suppressed arm geometry or an observation that sees through the carrier. [Bore audit](bore.json)

## Why this design

TT87 showed the old bracket blocked the actual ball ray on 50/55 training decision frames. TT93 first looked for compact seats whose aperture paths avoid the unchanged opaque moving jaw, wrist, and paddle layout. The best open paths then shaped the camera and its full supports. Of 92 compact sample aperture seats, 53 could expose at least seven moving-wrist training rays before adding carrier geometry. The selected opaque native build exposes 49/55 training rays and fits the ball, blade and jaw on at least two deadline frames in all 8 difficult training paths and 14/16 paths overall. All 55 moving-encoder poses pass the local module-to-shell CAD checks. The original static embedded foot joint is retained unchanged at −4.00 mm and is measured separately from the moving shell clearance.

The earliest blind cone-ranked drawing and fixed-foot comparison pilots are kept separately. The new seat was selected before seeing any changed-fixture receiving RGB. Incomplete native scouts for the earlier drawing were stopped before optical testing.

## Prospective evaluation

Twenty-four fresh, ordinary TT37 native first-offer draws supplied the first complete physical geometry quota. Four central lows, four wing lows, and four routine arrivals were selected before recording any RGB or receiver outcome. Fourteen independent launches are reserved for full-game testing if the physical observations support a causal camera correction. The qualification uses ordinary exterior-camera receiving on the **fully installed** sensor fixture and captures the unmodified native RGB of the wrist, left and right views. Wrist rays use only the encoder-calibrated lens and natural orange ball pixels. Scoring uses the achieved ball state and blade pose to compare contemporaneous bearings and triangulations on exact matched arrivals. The game fuser, if admitted, sees only camera data and time-stamped encoders.

Eleven of 12 fresh arrivals supplied at least two useful wrist RGB frames with an unclipped ball and visible blade between 67–150 ms before impact or virtual crossing. That includes all four low-center and seven of eight total hard shots. Local fitted module-to-shell checks observed no new penetration on the captured trajectories. On 25 paired 3-D frames, median errors were 0.829 mm with left+right, 1.109 mm with left+wrist, and 1.086 mm with all three. The close wrist view is a usable second triangulation baseline, but adding its ray indiscriminately degraded the already precise exterior stereo. A narrower causal transverse update from the wrist angle, which preserved the exterior range, reduced median paddle-plane error from 0.750 to 0.637 mm on matched deadline frames. That qualified the four paired new full points. It did not qualify a wrist-only policy.
