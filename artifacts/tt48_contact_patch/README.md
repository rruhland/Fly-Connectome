# Same-ball edge rescues on the stock arm

Three exact-state repairs turned four archived low, touched terminal misses
into three legal returns that the unchanged opponent answered. All three
rescues still used the outer edge of the 76 mm blade. The only tested
inner-face contact was at 57 mm and still failed. Steering the impact was
useful, but forcing it toward the blade center was not a prerequisite.

An unchanged-grid replication on four later, distinct measured releases
added one new same-ball rescue on 9114. It moved the native alignment
from .74 to .997 while keeping the contact at roughly 76 mm radius.
The fixed opponent answered the newly scored shot. Both control tapes
replayed exactly from launch with no penetrations in 6,610 full-CAD
solver samples. The other three probes still missed. This replicate was
not added to the three deployed treatment donors.

## Controlled practice and knowledge boundary

Each branch starts on a copy of the same physical solver state following
the far hit and before the target incoming bounce. The archived command
prefix and original failed suffix reproduce the archived referee events.
The grid varies aim point on the disk while sharing the incoming contact
phase, desired orientation families and target rates. Requested radii
do not necessarily equal actual radii. At each planned phase the exact
practice plant screens bounded direct reference changes relative to the
old tape. One physically tested candidate per observed radius band is
selected on the same nominal rollout cost. Bands are inner face below
59 mm and rim at 63–82 mm, not exact-center and not a proof of the
continuous reachable set. The comparison is a finite constructive
test on historically selected misses. Unchanged target draws, far
contacts and near-side bounces are checked against the original physical
delivery. Ball state and actual collision times in this diagnostic are
privileged training/evaluation inputs, not signals passed to a deployed
player.

## Optical transfer

The transfer proposes only a direct joint residual derived from the
actual issued contrast on one of the three legally answered practice
corrections. It does not replay any source tape. The ordinary season
controller runs first. If a stock legal route exists it remains untouched.
If the camera-initialized independent plant predicts a weak near-rim
departure, an additional native constraint inspection reads relative
impact-normal alignment in that independent plant. A few bounded time
ramps cover the learned motor changes. Only legal candidates that retain
normal alignment and survive at least one perturbed optical hypothesis
replace the original action. A bounded lease retains the motion through
its predicted contact; a measured outgoing ball restores ordinary
feedback. No true current opponent aim, simulator ball, live concealed
body state or future observation enters the actor.

The fixed evaluation uses all 24 season assessment assignments, eight
untrained measured releases and three independently redrawn broad streams
per release. The reference is the season-before matched frozen stock actor
with the same parent-48 memory, scene and rules. A cap of 240 alternating
returns is unchanged. [Fixed assessment and physically earned deltas](held.protocol.json)

## Why the camera twin missed the remedies

On the original known command tapes for 9230, 11809 and 9114, the
camera-initialized twin rejected all three actual edge rescues.
Substituting the exact achieved arm and paddle state left those
predictions unchanged to solver precision. Substituting exact ball
position, velocity and rotation restored all three scored edge shots
and all three stock failures without needing exact body state.
Substituting only ball rotation restored one edge shot but also
incorrectly counted its stock arm. On 11809 the camera model predicted
the edge action would leave at −3.29 m/s vertically. The exact ball
state restored +3.18 m/s, consistent with the +3.19 m/s actually
measured by RGB. None of these privileged substitutions enters the
actor. [Field-swap diagnostic](optical-factors.json)

The same three issued command pairs gave no correct stock-fail, edge-score
ranking under archived flight fits, early-bounce fit, or actual 60 Hz stereo.
The archived dense estimator did not issue an estimate at these pre-bounce
samples. Alternate camera states also tend to favor the wrong action.

## Synchronized dense stereo and action deadline

Sampling the same lenses at 60 Hz with a 4× wider and taller pixel grid,
using actual RGB, a wider pre-bounce capture window and a fitted smooth
flight with optional transverse curvature, distinguished one of the three
fixed command pairs. Position error on that offer was 0.42 mm and
velocity error was 0.009 m/s. The optically fitted 9114 state was even
closer translationally but still rejected both commands. Its fitted spin
remained about 6 rad/s off. Neither this specific selected-offer result
nor the earlier uncertainty vote establishes a deployable policy.

The separate release-disjoint sensor holdout kept eight predetermined
recorded tapes, scored their first two receiving arcs, and preserved the
verified native prefixes. On 65 pre-bounce samples, the 4× area/shape arc

A separate fit used synchronized pre-/post-bounce centers to constrain
the angular state in a calibrated private ball/table model. It recovered
one physically viable delayed choice on 11809, already predicted by
the dense pre-bounce prior without fitting a rebound correction.
It did not recover 9230 or 9114 before their action deadlines. The fit
often explained the observed positions much better without estimating
angular state well enough to improve command ranking. One immediate

with those evaluator fields. These are bounds on this private initializer,
not licenses to see physical state inside the player.

## Signed RGB evidence override

The two same-delivery sets yield eight scored or failed rim contrasts
on eight different practice releases. A post-impact stereo estimate
was recoverable on all eight edge actions and six stock actions.
No native departure was used to fill a missing optical label.
The learned correction estimates the signed difference between a
camera-initialized virtual departure and the measured RGB departure.
Matching supports must include both real scored and failed movements.
When the original virtual stroke is weak, a new candidate can bypass
nominal ball-flight rejection only if its evidence-adjusted outgoing
arc is plausible. The negative control reverses this signed residual
while keeping all supports and thresholds fixed.

Leave-one-release-out on that selected practice set, the learned rule
recommended two actual rescues and no failures, compared with zero
rescues for the reversed direction. Departure RMSE was 1.95 m/s,
versus 1.99 without correction and 2.13 for reversal. Permitting all
three structurally eligible archived alternatives would have yielded
three rescues, so this is evidence for direction versus reversal,
not evidence that the signed gate is superior to permissive use.

In the separate native pilot on four untrained measured openings,
none of the learned or reverse variants met the issuance gate. All
three missed offers remained misses, and all arms reproduced the one
productive control opening exactly. This does not establish online
transfer. The pilot does not change the separate sealed season run.
