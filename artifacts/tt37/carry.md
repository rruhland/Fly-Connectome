# Can the arm reuse what worked in earlier rallies?

On twelve distinct measured openings, the arm made 14 legal returns with memory frozen empty, 26 while storing physically scored actions from these games, and 32 with additional memory of 86 earlier successes. Each incoming target was drawn independently. The simulated opponent had to intercept the previous return to create the next one. The longest point in the cold learning run contained nine arm returns and ten opposing returns. [Opponent and court](opponent.md) · [Twelve assigned openings](school-plan.json)

| Frozen empty memory | 8 | 6 | 3 / 7 | 179 |

| Carry 86 earlier successes and continue learning | 10 | 22 | 6 / 13 | 74 |

The controller first tests nearby motor corrections that previously earned a legal landing. It simulates them in a separate nominal robot initialized from camera observations, the arm's encoder angles and the setpoints it has actually issued. If none of those alternatives is predicted to land, it searches additional joint corrections. Only actual table landings update motor memory. Its geometric stroke generator and nominal physics are supplied priors. All three arms started from the same joint angles and used the same simulation budgets and randomization rule. Across whole games their simulated time differed because better play kept the ball alive. Total CPU time was 59.8, 82.4 and 89.4 seconds for 45.3, 66.3 and 73.3 seconds of simulated play, respectively. These timings include stereo rendering, opponent and arena physics. ## Learning a correction from subsequent camera frames

The learner can update its visual estimate on scoreless frames. Every target uses a later observation of the ball, and the update becomes available three frames after the source observation. Learning how the ball rebounds from 140 observed practice bounces reduced vertical velocity error to 0.041 m/s on 283 held receiving frames, from 0.366 m/s originally. An additional free-flight smoother increased that error to 0.051 m/s. The original calibrated rebound estimator remained the default for the memory comparison. [Learned rebound](rebound-quality.json) · [Smoother](flight-fit-quality.json)

At 33 early physical decision states we also replayed 39 candidate joint commands per state and compared predictions against native outcomes. For the exact candidates proposed from the improved sensor, the optical body model recognized 170 of 384 legal candidates, with 229 predicted legal. Replacing the sensed translation and velocity with the true values recognized 325, and also providing spin recognized all 384. At seven later decision states, the optical body model recognized 61 of 75 legal alternatives and predicted 103 legal. These are grouped diagnostics on practice states. They explain why the learner can choose a stroke it believes will land and still miss. [Matched native predictions and substitutions](replay-gated.json.gz)

## What transferred into camera-to-joint feedback

On 24 held releases, the 289-return direct pupil landed 16 total arm returns. Adding demonstrations from the same 14 practice starts in two ways produced different effects. Extra coach-controlled practice produced 19 returns on the 24 new openings and 20 on a separate set of 16 familiar openings with new replies. Letting the pupil approach the ball itself before the coach intervened produced 13 and 28 on those same courses. A shift in the practice poses benefited the familiar openings and cost performance on new ones. The design follows the observation that a learner visits different states under its own actions. [Training on learner-visited states](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html) · [Matched coach demonstrations](matched-teacher-rgb.json) · [Matched intervention demonstrations](matched-rollin-rgb.json)

A second direct learner predicted overlapping sequences of joint targets and averaged estimates for the current tick. On the 24 novel releases it produced 12 returns versus 16 for single-tick feedback, while on the 16 familiar openings it produced 20 versus 15. Calibrating joint commands against the effect they actually have on the paddle remains a higher priority. ## What changed during a single point

Clearing learned motor memory before each of the twelve openings preserved all eight first successes of the frozen-empty controller. Legal later returns rose from six to thirteen. On release 15294, learning during that point extended three arm returns to seven. On 13250 the same rule shortened three to one. Letting learned actions carry between points gave seventeen later returns. With 86 previous successes, freezing the motor memory gave 23 total returns and continuing to learn gave 32. On a separately assigned course of 24 unfamiliar openings, the empty-frozen controller made 43 total returns, learning from empty made 39, and carrying the 26 actions learned in the twelve-point course also made 39. Motor-memory gains depend on the distribution. A separate non-backprop readout learned how often a movement predicted to land in the private simulator actually scored and elicited a reply. It fit 197 previously issued actions on 32 release IDs. The test group of twelve had zero overlapping IDs. The input describes the candidate's predicted landing, net clearance, speed and blade contact radius. Earlier real rewards supply the labels. Using the readout to rank otherwise identical candidate sets raised legal arm returns from 32 to 66, with at least one return on all twelve games and a longest point of 15 arm returns, 31 alternating. It made 4,951 simulated alternatives for the 66 physical returns, about 75 per return. On the independent 24 games it made 46 total, with a longest point of five. On 24 previously unseen releases, adding 70 camera-controlled, physically scored practice returns to the direct joint pupil's existing 449-return training set raised total arm returns from 17 to 39. Both policies converted nine openings. Legal later returns rose from 8 to 30. On release 3298 one point rose from two arm returns to eleven, 23 alternating. The 70 demonstrations came from a separate camera/encoder physics controller, and the resulting direct pupil uses no counterfactual simulation at play time. Training examples were identified by replaying physically scored contacts. On the independent 24-release course, starting both motor memory and the movement-quality predictor empty and updating both after actual strokes produced 68 legal returns across 16 openings, with 18 in the longest point. Holding both empty yielded 43 across 16 openings, with eight in the longest point. Carrying the earlier learned predictor and motor prior while continuing to update them scored 53 across 17 openings. The cold learner began 0, 0, 1, 10 and later had several more scoreless points. These comparisons show better subsequent returns without a monotonic progression on every new opening. The scorer uses known nominal physics, calibrated stereo and a geometric movement prior. Checking the current motion against newly observed camera frames during approach improved the twelve-game total from 66 to 81. All twelve openings scored, and the longest individual point changed from 15 to 14. On a second 24-opening course the raw direct pupil with the 70 extra physical demonstrations fell from 20 to 14 legal returns. Updating those direct commands during play raised 14 to 19 on that course, while on the first 24 it reduced 39 to 33. These are important failures of consistent transfer. Preparing the arm with a learned direct joint field before letting the physics controller strike produced 104 legal returns from 126 incoming legal deliveries on the independent 24 games. The same physics controller and motor/reward prior using its original preparation scored 46 of 67. The change raised openings with a return from 17 to 18 and increased later returns from 29 to 86. These games were disjoint from the direct preparatory field’s training releases. The same field used only after the physics controller ran out of strike alternatives scored 49, making early movement the more promising use. A separate reward model that forecasts two subsequent arm returns increased the total to 91 on the original preparation with a learned prior, but with an empty prior it scored 48 versus 68 for immediate outcomes.
