# Retaining a touch and refining the stroke

The signed camera observer recovered and retained a touching reach on both tested openings, but eight new late adjustments per opening produced no legal return. On 6861 the measured ball left the blade moving upward after refinement, and the physical bounce moved 27.6 cm closer to the net. It still landed on the near half. On 6253 the ball continued toward the arm. [Checkpoint](impact-refine-checkpoint.json) · [Per-game tapes](impact-refine.json.gz) · [Physical audit](impact-refine-audit.json)

The base policy, posture and early control were the raw-camera joint pupil with the prior contextual reach correction. For each opening, we scored four existing inverse action tapes with camera evidence and kept the observed contact closest to an outgoing velocity learned from previously rewarded games. This retained attempt 10 on 6253 and attempt 9 on 6861. The first survived the fixed 60 Hz stereo motion-change observer. The second needed an occlusion fallback because the right camera lost sight of the ball. The fallback fits a change in signed velocity using rays from the still-visible eye, stereo observations before and after, and the measured blade pose. It rejects a change unless a near-blade bend explains the observations substantially better than unchanged flight. For 6861 attempt 9, it used 13 monocular frames and three stereo points after reappearance. It estimated `(−0.182, +2.582, −0.341)` m/s versus the independently audited `(−0.188, +2.521, −0.418)` m/s. The estimate took 217 ms after contact. This gap recovery was tested on the two local families, not included in the release-disjoint stereo benchmark. [Prior stereo benchmark](impact.md) · [Implementation](../../scripts/experimental/tt36_impact_refine.py)

Each family received exactly eight new games, with up to 0.43 radians added to the joint-angle command as the camera-observed ball neared the paddle position estimated from encoders. In each game the controller first selected its stored movement by optical/proprioceptive proximity. It did not receive a release ID. The search used five bounded probes followed by three local updates based on the displacement of the measured departure velocity. It ranked actual legal returns first, then closeness to the previously rewarded velocities. Physical impact labels did not enter selection. It selected late attempts 6 and 8 respectively. All 16 new games physically touched the ball and the observer detected 14. All scored zero legal returns.

We froze the checkpoint and ran two unused opponent continuation seeds per opening for the prior, retained-only and refined movements. All three conditions received the exact same physical first ball in each comparison. The prior missed 4/4, retaining restored 4/4 touches, and refining kept 4/4. All three made zero legal returns, so no second reply was delivered and this test does not establish transfer to different live shots. Retained and refined issued identical commands until ticks 83 and 75 for 6253 and 6861, respectively, at 60 control ticks per second. Six representative held tapes exactly reproduced their referee events and placements on replay. The CAD audit sampled 5,183 solver states with zero penetration and 17.0 mm minimum clearance.
