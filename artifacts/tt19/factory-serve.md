# Replace SO-101 serving practice with factory-model proposals

A server chosen entirely in a separate factory-model simulation won both new native RGB games 11–7 with the existing rollout receiver. It served legally on 15 of 16 own attempts. This replaced the previous server’s 1,004 target practice points and 38,526 control frames on the tested compact station. Two 12-attempt correction procedures selected the same different candidate. That candidate lost one paired game 9–11 and won the other 11–8. The server starts with canonical joint coordinates from the published SO-101 geometry and the nominal gripped paddle. It proposes tool paths around a legal descending toss and converts each to a timed motor sweep. The independent simulator uses the same nominal plant, actuator increments, compact court and contact parameters as the native target fixture. Its ball launches come from independent seeds and a wider distribution around public serving geometry. Every launch starts behind the end line. The sampled positions span −0.070 to +0.072 m laterally, a −0.929 to −0.863 m longitudinal design range, a 0.316 to 0.383 m height range above the table, and a 2.67 to 3.035 m/s upward speed range. It uses no target stroke bank or target trajectory for proposal generation. Its collision paths supplied an explicit score for own bounce, net clearance and the far bounce, with opponent-racket contact disabled. The server search took 432 virtual tapes of 51 control frames each, or 22,032 factory-model frames, and approximately 5.8 seconds on the host. Each shortlisted motion made five nominally legal serves across the eight synthetic scenarios. The factory-only branch froze the highest ranked proposal before any target practice. Native play used wrist and fixed stereo RGB to choose lateral aim. The receiving branch retained the same independent dry factory catalogue, rollout ranker, and gantry flight and impact priors across the four games. The factory rollout costs and the previously learned gantry priors are still part of this configuration. ## Blind replication and controlled prior comparison

Two additional native full games used the same saved continual checkpoint and the same twelve distinct practice attempts. The factory stroke lost 10–12 and 3–11. The target-corrected stroke lost 10–12 and 1–11. Across all four games, the zero-practice player won two, made 31/33 legal serves, and made 36 legal returns, including five points with at least two. The corrected player won one game, made 27/36 legal serves, and returned 37 balls, also with five multiple-return points. All 70 new physical tapes replayed. [Blind schedule one](factory-serve-82617492-distinct-held.json.gz) · [Blind schedule two](factory-serve-335280197-distinct-held.json.gz) · [Combined accounting](factory-serve-summary.json.gz)

With the same zero-target-practice server on all four schedules, the nominal-prior control won 11–8 and 11–9 and lost 6–11 and 3–11. Gantry and nominal priors each won 2/4 and served legally 31/33. The gantry priors yielded 36 legal returns across 72 point attempts and five points with multiple returns. Nominal priors yielded 25 across 70 attempts and two multiple-return points. The carried player legally returned the opening rival serve on 28/39 opportunities and the nominal player on 22/37. Different point outcomes can change service order and game length. This comparison cannot attribute first-game wins to the gantry. On 24 identical receiving opportunities the first-return difference was two starts.
