# Learning SO-101 strokes and scoring complete games

The compact-table player wins full games on novel launches after practicing with the SO-101. Its strongest skill is serving. Winning a point may take one shot. We also count each learner shot that legally lands on the far table, and each point with two such returns. An own serve is scored separately and requires the two service bounces. A ball that reaches a racket but fails to land legally earns no return. The rival can hit a ball long, miss a return or fault on the next exchange. The corrected end-line referee faults a long shot once the whole ball passes the receiving end without the required bounce. Early trials using the legacy rule remain labeled in their own reports. ## What was learned

Each motion program specifies a starting joint displacement, subsequent swing, timing and lateral aim adjustment. For each role, practice proposes bounded programs, tries them against different incoming balls and records public reward plus a placement estimate from optical motion. The estimate recognizes progress towards the far table and an apparent bounce. After several generations and repeated trials, the policy weights the performance of each program on incoming balls with similar lateral position and height. A recovering player can use a separate public dry-model catalogue to plan another hit. The fitted parameters and stroke choices persist across points. During the reported held games the stroke libraries were frozen, so the scores measure deployment from learned experience. [Conditioned practice](../../scripts/experimental/tt19_contextual.py) · [Qualification and retrieval](../../scripts/experimental/tt19_consolidate.py) · [Practice at different serve spreads](../../scripts/experimental/tt19_feeder_learning.py)

Practice sped up simulation by drawing the colored ball into two calibrated camera views. This gives practice a clean ball image with no occlusion. The closed-loop native tests render the full MuJoCo scene with the actual wrist camera plus two fixed cameras. Visible surveyed colored landmarks, known approximate ball dimensions and colored blade markings support optical geometry. The training heuristic uses table coordinates and knowledge of legal shot direction. The learned placement signal, supplied motion programs and public factory model are substantial scaffolding. No network gradients or backpropagation were used. [Fast practice sensor](../../scripts/experimental/tt19_practice_camera.py) · [Native RGB observations](views.md)

Launching generic timed strokes selected only by point reward gave zero legal returns across six held games. Adding RGB-conditioned placement and testing each promising movement repeatedly produced close full-spread games, including 11–13 and 10–12. The later libraries started from the best strokes in independently practiced local and gantry-informed branches, then qualified them again. Both branches represent real SO-101 practice. For the narrower feeder only the opening launch distribution changes. Lateral release variation was scaled to 20%, the other launch variation to 35%. The table, scoring, arm and rival play after the launch stay the same. The native direct full-spread and narrow players have separate checkpoints. ## Transfer and retention

A ball-flight model fitted from 408 gantry RGB windows forecast arm-game ball positions on 115 held windows. Median error at 3, 6 and 10 frames was 11.7, 23.2 and 49.1 mm. Constant velocity gave 99.6, 295.7 and 688.8 mm. A supplied nominal physical model already gave 11.2, 20.9 and 46.2 mm. Fitting the arm table gave 9.9, 18.5 and 41.3 mm. Thus the old learned model supplies useful knowledge from the first frame, while the known physical model is an equally strong starting point in this simulator. [Flight model and test windows](flight-transfer.json.gz)

A second test started the arm motor map cold, charged both players 24 setup actions and held the remaining fixture fixed. The gantry-informed player served legally at frame 184. The cold player had no legal serve; both made one legal return, at frames 63 and 58 respectively. Substituting the supplied ball law alone for the gantry fit delayed the warm player's legal serve to frame 448. Pong gains affected 26/36 action tapes but left exactly the same six successful first returns. Actual gantry point reward also failed to select better receiving strokes after 12 or 24 target practice points. [Motor transfer](online.md) · [Native Pong control](pong-forward.md) · [Gantry utility control](utility-transfer.md)

The winning checkpoints carry the original body and game policies. During an earlier online variant, pooling the new table's bounce and impulse estimates cut gantry returns from 18 to five on the same test. Selecting the gantry's own stored estimates restored all 18 command and image tapes. Task and body routing use a known address in these retention tests. This shows preservation with explicit routing. Automatic recognition on returning to an old body remains open. [Continual comparison and checkpoint-access audit](retention.md)

The practiced factory-model receiver provides a second type of forward-transfer test. With zero extra target calibration on the held games, replacing only the learned gantry flight and impact priors with nominal values turned the 13–11 win into a 6–11 loss. Across that four-game comparison, prior experience produced 34 legal returns and five points with multiple returns versus 29 and three for nominal. The gantry player won one game and the nominal player lost all four. Totals span different numbers of scored points because games end at different scores. The paired direct-receive test isolates performance on identical opening opportunities. [Matched complete-game control](model-rollout.md)
