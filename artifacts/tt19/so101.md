# SO-101 follower benchmark

TT19 puts the stock-scale SO-101 follower at one end of a 1.600 × 0.848 m table with a 0.760 m playing surface and the 0.1525 m TT8 net. The follower is fixed to a 0.490 m pedestal. A scripted TT8 rival with its original arm mechanism sits at the far end. Its launch and interception are adjusted to the smaller table. Both serve roles, floor and net faults, deuce and scoring to 11 retain the TT8 rules. The ball is 40 mm in diameter and weighs 2.7 g. [Model and fixture](../../scripts/experimental/tt19_so101.py)

The source is TheRobotStudio's new-calibration follower model with the optional 32 × 32 mm camera assembly. The benchmark retains the upstream inter-link transforms, six hinge axes and ranges, body inertias, centers of mass, and motor gains and torque limits. The six actuator targets update at 30 Hz. They clip at the upstream limits. The simulator's compliant joint stops can give slightly under load, as in the source model. The measured source-derived masses are 147 g for the base, 100.006 g for the shoulder, 103 g and 104 g for the upper and lower arms, 79 g for the wrist, 87 g for the gripper housing, and 12 g for the moving jaw. The source camera and mount add 15 g and 20 g. These figures come from the CAD-derived simulator parameters. [Pinned upstream model](https://github.com/TheRobotStudio/SO-ARM100/blob/141aea7bcac2744d92998e30268c413202a56f96/Simulation/SO101/so101_new_calib_camera.xml) · [Asset attribution and license](../../scripts/experimental/tt19_so101_assets/NOTICE.md)

The 115 g paddle is a free rigid body with a 76 mm-radius blade and a handle between the real rotating jaws. A no-slip constraint holds the pre-grasped handle. Opening the measured jaw angle beyond 0.44 radians releases it into ordinary rigid-body dynamics. The fixture starts with a held paddle and disables arm self-collisions. The paddle, ball, net, table and floor use actual MuJoCo contacts. The simulated camera optics are set to see the jaw and blade and move with the original camera housing. Station placement, paddle attachment, optics and control-rate caps are benchmark choices.

## What the physical audit reproduced

- The exact-CAD and the reduced-display meshes produced bit-identical joint and ball states, point scores and contact logs on eight command tapes. The reduced meshes contain 32,093 triangles across the source assets, versus 328,617 in the original. Use the full CAD for source fidelity or `detail='preview'` when rendering long trials.
- All 64 sampled opponent launches legally bounced on both halves. A separate evaluator-directed command tape produced a legal return that landed 0.292 m past the net and won the receiving point. A representative learner-side toss also produced a legal scored serve. These witnesses establish physical reach and scoring.
- Five movement samples kept the paddle within 0.03 mm of its prescribed position in the gripper frame. Opening the actual jaw released it at frame 10, and its height subsequently fell by half a metre.
- The native wrist RGB image resolved the moving jaw and the blade simultaneously. Three normalized commands moved the camera center by 74 mm. The close-up uses a 12 mm near clip to include the closely mounted jaw geometry. `wrist_near=.012` enables that clip without modifying the fixed stereo sensors.
