# What the SO-101 carried from bouncing into table tennis

The arm learned how to compensate for a paddle that sat differently in its gripper. With only wrist RGB, joint encoders, and rebound rewards, it found a useful adjustment when all five joints were available. Reusing that discovery improved legal returns in a full-size receiving game. Experience also changed which joint it explored first on four new grips. The existing table-tennis movement repertoire still came from six earlier simulator-assisted demonstrations. ## How the solo stage worked

We physically rotated the simulated collision blade by ±0.22 radians about its handle. The amount and sign were hidden from the learner. The standard grip was included as a separate control. The policy began with the repository's earlier wrist-vision and five-joint control weights. We varied release, spin, drag, contact damping and servo strength. The five-joint search tried the neutral setting and positive and negative adjustments for every joint, in randomly interleaved order. Each of the 110 attempts per grip had a fresh launch. Accumulated rebound rewards selected an additive joint adjustment. Both changed grips independently selected wrist roll in opposite directions. The standard grip selected zero. No new gradient or backpropagation step was used. [Five-joint choices, practice seeds and held scores](five-joint-practice.json.gz)

Simply adding the chosen offset to motor commands left a mismatch in perception. The arm still estimated the blade angle from its old grip. We used the existing encoder-to-link calibration to infer the change in tool attitude implied by the successful adjustment. The controller then used that attitude when interpreting the paddle in its wrist view. On 24 independent, physically released balls, the median streak rose from one uncorrected to two with motor compensation alone and 24 with motor and attitude updated together. The maximum was 29 in eight seconds. These trials varied initial posture, release location, ball mass, drag, and gravity. All three conditions had the same short preparation interval and the same inherited camera controller. Their action tapes reproduced the referee outcomes. ## What went into the receiving game

The receiving arm used four frames from its fixed camera pair to choose a movement. It controlled physical joint servos on the collision-enabled, full-size court. The two games have separate visual policies and share the discovered five-axis setting. The existing receiving options were acquired from six simulator-assisted examples on a standard grip. The experiments test transfer of tool use and learning efficiency given those options. A separate practice search with seven wrist settings and five paired source drops per setting selected the same ±0.24-radian corrections. Its shuffled control permuted rewards among the available actions. [Source schedule](grip-source.json.gz) · [Court calibration and previous examples](../tt25/recorded-benchmarks.md)

* On 72 previously unseen randomized feeds per grip, unrestricted five-joint selection produced **44 versus 11** legal returns with the negative mounting angle and **42 versus 21** with the positive angle. Both versions scored **44/72** on the standard grip. The environment perturbed six recorded real launches to create a beginner course. The actor saw the rendered ball and its own joints. [Five-joint receiving scores](five-joint-match.json.gz)
* On 144 distinct, unaltered recordings from the vicinity of the six examples, evaluated with each changed grip, the aggregate was **23/288 versus 7/288**. Both scored **0/64** when evaluated under both grips on a separate set of 32 unrestricted recorded shots. Some launches ended before the fourth camera observation. They remain in the denominator. [Recorded-shot scores](grip-match.json.gz)
* Eight chronological target-learning streams used four independent shot rosters crossed with the two grips. Each learner updated a setting from legal landings, with equal scheduled exploration. Bounce practice reached the fifth legal return sooner in all eight pairs. The median was eight target shots versus 18 cold and 17.5 with shuffled source rewards. The final frozen block gave 65/128, 53/128 and 48/128, respectively. Revisiting the original solo seeds after restoring the checkpoints reproduced the learned bounce scores for all eight streams. Source practice is additional experience, so this comparison measures savings in **table-tennis shots**. [Online histories](receiving-speed.json.gz) · [Reload and retention](retention.json.gz)
* To test learning the actual visual decision, we erased all six camera-to-action associations, randomized action labels and trained a local reward memory for 96 shots. Across four streams, the prepared arms subsequently returned **76/192 (40%)** fresh beginner feeds versus **43/192 (22%)** without the preparation and 1/192 with shuffled settings. Two streams share each underlying shot roster. All learners had access to the same six anonymous motor options. [Actions, camera descriptors and score histories](visual-acquisition.json.gz)
* We played out 48 new paired points with a physical opponent, across two grips and 24 unique opening variations. The opponent returned every one of the prepared arm's 28 legal first balls. The learner missed each reply. The source adjustment helps start a rally. Recovery for the next ball remains the limit on sustained match play. Every complete two-arm point passed independent motor-tape replay. [Full points](live-points.json.gz) · [Video verification](bounce-to-table.json)

### A carried checkpoint and return to solo play

On a fresh pair of 96-shot practice streams, the five-joint solo search fed its actual winning adjustment to a joint checkpoint. The player discarded the incoming-camera labels, learned them from target scores, saved itself after shot 96 and resumed for assessment. It returned **44/96** fresh beginner feeds versus **24/96** cold. On returning to solo play, all 16 new wrist-camera starts reproduced their entire pre-match command sequence and rebound score exactly. Eight of the 16 reached at least 20 consecutive rebounds. The two visual heads are separate and both tasks read the same saved tool adjustment. This tests a practical form of continual learning with an explicit task switch. ## Learning how to explore on an unfamiliar grip

We discarded the learned correction completely. The only information carried into the new setting was a ranking of which joints' positive and negative adjustments changed solo rewards. On four previously unseen physical grips of ±0.15 and ±0.30 radians, a new receiving pupil started at zero and tried adjustments using table-tennis rewards. At every budget, controls had evaluated the same number of candidates on the same feeds. The uniform comparison averages all 120 joint orders. A second control estimates orders from 128 permutations of solo reward labels. Eight varied practice feeds were used per candidate. The common held set contained 24 separate beginner feeds per grip. Four practice packets were tested per condition.

The bounce-informed order reached **50% held returns after 24 target probes**. Uniform exploration averaged **26%** at 24 and reached 50% at 88, once it had tried all five joints. Three altered grips benefited early, and the fourth and the neutral control already did as well without an adjustment. The 24 held variants were reused across grips and orderings. This result applies to grip rotations around the handle. [All measured actions and budget curves](exploration-transfer.json.gz)

## Other transfer mechanisms

A motor predictor fitted from bounce-time encoders reduced mean angular tracking error on a lagged servo test from 19.21 to 17.91 milliradians. Legal returns stayed tied. More aggressive inverse feedback performed worse. An independently optimized varied-drop actor doubled wrist-camera rebound counts on the varied course and reduced performance on ordinary drops. [Motor prediction, tracking, and the source tradeoff](bounce.md)

Fitting paddle restitution from physically verified rebounds matched the existing cold reflection estimate on the standard paddle. It gave identical held results. On a softer paddle, cold returned two nearby shots and the fitted variant returned none. This fit used simulator impact measurements during teaching. Those trials provide no evidence of target gains from that particular collision model. [Collision-learning experiments](match-rebound.md)
