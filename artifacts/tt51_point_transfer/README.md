# What did the 63-return rally teach the next game?

Both copies faced the same 22 unfamiliar releases and independent opponent streams, with their memory frozen during assessment. The first carried the exact memory at entry to the 63-return point. The second carried its memory at exit. Its 272 newly earned movement fragments replaced the oldest 272 of 768. The overlapping fragments and original per-point memory fingerprints were checked when reconstructing those checkpoints. Four points lengthened, eight shortened and ten tied. The paired bootstrap interval for the mean change was −7.59 to +1.45 returns per game. The first different command occurred on an identical physical delivery in all 18 games with a different command. Both arms returned that same ball in 17 cases and missed it in one. Different successful strokes can lead to different subsequent opponent responses. These totals measure how long the games lasted. They do not isolate same-ball loss of competence at the eventual terminal offer. ## Replacing old movements and adding new movements

On those same 22 points, deleting the oldest 272 fragments while adding nothing made 121 returns. Adding the 272 newly earned fragments while preserving all 768 old ones made 137. Using the actual replacement bank made 130, compared with 196 from the original. Limiting the number of proposals the long point contributed at each decision made 107. Further capacity alone did not recover the original performance on this schedule. [All five conditions and first-to-last point scores](interference.json)

Fourteen terminal-ball cross-plays then changed the motor bank just after the actual opponent's racket released the ball. Neither direction rescued any terminal offer. In six of the fourteen cases commands changed. Across the seven pairs with a delivered ball, the two original body positions at this cut differed by at most 0.061 mm at the blade center. In five pairs the ball itself followed a different trajectory. This directs attention to what the arm sends back before the eventual terminal ball.
