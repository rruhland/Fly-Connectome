# Grounding the planar controller in the wrist interface

The stock SO-101 received the frozen wrist geometry and motion estimators from `bounce-ident-pupil.npz`. These estimators were calibrated with privileged pose and motion labels on four earlier teacher-driven drops. The new trial explicitly loaded only decoder and motion arrays. Motor learning used self-issued joint rates, simultaneous wrist and encoder readings, scalar rebound reward, and elapsed time. [Sensory calibration provenance](bounce-ident.md) · [Experiment](../../scripts/experimental/tt24_ground_trial.py)

The positive source treatment carried the mean of eight powered-planar pupils' two-axis action logits and the original Pong visual-value estimate. The shuffle treatment used the corresponding planar pupils trained with shuffled native source labels and the shuffled estimate. The cold treatment used zero source logits and a flat source signal. For the port, the decoded axes were assumed gravity-aligned with positive z up. The same planar head read each horizontal–vertical projection. Its two vertical suggestions were averaged. Normalization used the nominal 7.6 cm paddle half-span and explicit bounded speed or displacement scales. This supplied orientation and size convention is part of the tested bridge.

Each treatment trained a new five-joint motor mapping from four common exploratory drops. The initial version fitted short commanded-rate blocks to observed paddle velocity and tilt. The second version fitted small realized encoder changes to simultaneous decoded paddle displacement and tilt, using a bounded correction with the initial tilt as its reference. The four probes were full physical drops and were charged to each treatment. The initial version then tried twelve scored drops, and its independent pose-map replacement tried eight. Both used two practice seeds in their scored blocks and twelve separate held seeds. A residual sensory motor readout could adapt from actual rebound count and duration. The pose version included a zero-command candidate alongside the transported controls.

| Velocity, four probes + twelve scored drops | 0.58 | 1.67 | 1.00 | 2.83 |

The means are referee-verified rebounds per held drop. In both rows the live mean also equalled mean longest consecutive run. The initial velocity fit used 35 blocks, had normalized in-sample RMS error 4.60, and its largest-to-smallest measured gain ratio was about 165. In that cohort, even the cold controller disrupted passive behavior. The replacement pose fit had a measured gain ratio of about 10.5. Both source treatments selected zero commands in the pose experiment and reproduced the stationary result on all twelve held cases. Every active powered proposal scored fewer rebounds in its practice pair than the neutral candidate. The cold treatment chose an active residual, with 2.00 held rebounds. All 192 held command tapes reproduced the physics judge. These bounded trials leave active short-budget transfer to the arm unresolved even with the supplied calibrated wrist representation.
