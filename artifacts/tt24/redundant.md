# Action-effect transfer to six actuated joints

A new independently actuated wrist bracket adds a sixth arm joint before the gripper. Its pivot is about 32 mm along the original distal assembly and its axis is slightly oblique to the original pitch axis. The new component carries mass and separate actuator dynamics. The old wrist-flex target increments run at 1.72 rad/s, and the distal actuator can run at 5.85 rad/s. The jaw keeps its fixed grasp. The pinned control uses the same body and a joint equality constraint at the added hinge. [Arm model](../../scripts/experimental/tt24_redundant.py)

The unmodified stock wrist pupil supplied the skill, including its earlier teacher-calibrated perception and motor readout. The new body supplied self-issued rates, encoder readings, and common optical landmarks on the paddle from two fixed calibration cameras. The transfer used the legacy five encoder indices and their reset angles as the known correspondence. It fitted the sixth joint's contribution to the inherited encoder coordinates from observed images, then identified the action mapping that reproduced the source body’s measured motor effects. The six-joint map used **12 balanced unlabelled probe blocks in one reset**, 180 control frames or 3.0 seconds of simulated motion. The stock and pinned mappings each used ten blocks and 150 frames. All blocks had usable marker measurements. The calibration runs disabled ball contact and supplied no reward or desired path. Held play used native wrist RGB and own encoders, with the external cameras and paddle markers excluded from the policy. [Calibration and game runner](../../scripts/experimental/tt24_redundant_trial.py)

The learned moving-six controller exceeded the padded controller on four drops, tied on six, and trailed on two. Two large gains account for most of the 20 additional rebounds. The sixth motor's normalized rate RMS was 0.405. The mechanically pinned comparator performed better than the moving-six controller on ten drops. This fixture establishes transfer with genuine use of the extra actuator and leaves a game benefit from releasing that actuator unsupported. All 60 held action tapes reproduced the corresponding physics judge, including rebound events. [Calibration and full game receipts](redundant-game.json.gz)

A separate optical reaching test reused the same effects maps. Each goal was a fresh, independently executed stock-body optical displacement. The target received the measured endpoint displacement and six command ticks followed by nine settling ticks. No source path or target demonstration entered the receiving controller. Goal errors are RMS pixels across both calibration cameras at the endpoint. One of twenty goals lost a marker under the padded controller, leaving nineteen complete paired comparisons. Mean errors were **1.67 pixels** for the learned moving-six controller, 3.75 padded, 9.66 stationary, and 1.57 learned with the hinge pinned. The moving-six learner used a median sixth displacement of 0.088 rad per complete paired goal. The extra hinge did not improve these stock-derived reaches relative to the pinned learner.
