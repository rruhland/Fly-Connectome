# Can the learner identify a more promising body position before it can find a legal return?

The candidate mechanism was to predict which state a five-tick joint movement would reach, then move toward a state with a higher historical chance of returning the ball. Its prerequisite failed on 16,081 actual scored and missed decision windows from 133 practice releases. With opening recordings held out, adding the installed joint targets and their recent motion worsened the return predictor's Brier score from .0777 to .0798. Lower is better. [First comparison](fit.json)

A second comparison estimated actual encoder angles from recently issued references before computing the relative ball and paddle geometry. The fitted observation delay roughly halved the held encoder error on each of five joints. Adding the resulting blade geometry still worsened Brier score from .0755 to .0796. In the middle of the approach, the scores were .0704 and .0762. Correlations from ordinary play did not justify moving the arm to a different position on the strength of this readout. The separately tested interventions use actual matched motor changes on the same incoming ball.
