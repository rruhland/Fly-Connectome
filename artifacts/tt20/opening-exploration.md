# Reward-only stroke exploration with one camera

The first pilot used three native RGB frames before issuing an opening stroke. Each role had a library of earlier SO-101 movements with three timing choices. A contextual bandit updated the values of these choices after each point from its signed reward. It saw the orange ball's 2D screen position and motion, centered and scaled by earlier shots on the active camera. The two canonical image axes and a lateral aim rule were provided. This policy goes neutral after one stroke. The moving arm, natural red paddle, orange ball, unmarked compact court, scorer and opponent were shared across views.

Each camera got 100 own serves and 100 opponent serves. Outside-only training won zero points and made one first return. Wrist-only training won one point and made three first returns. Neither made a legal own serve. The initial wrist snapshots missed the ball on all 100 own serves and saw it on all 100 opposing serves. The later full-game experiments use longer optical histories and a newly simulated stroke library for the moved clamp.

On 24 fresh opponent serves interleaved with 24 own serves, transferring wrist practice to the outside camera made one return. Blanking the destination visual cue made five. Transferring outside practice to the wrist made zero returns before local practice. After eight fresh attempts per role it made two, versus zero with the destination cue blanked. Both transfer directions produced zero legal serves. These results give little evidence that this bandit learned an effective visual policy. Learning remained reward-starved even though the arm physically reached the ball. The sensors, command sequence and scorer can be checked against the archived checkpoint and digests.
