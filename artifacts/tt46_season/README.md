## What persists after further practice

Freezing the learner after its first 24 new practice games produced 135 legal arm returns from 159 delivered balls on the same 24 held-out games where the starting checkpoint produced 189 from 213. Median rally length fell from six to 3.5. Seven games improved, twelve shortened and five tied. Each test point ran to completion, so the number of balls offered depends on both earlier players' actions. The paired bootstrap interval on the mean change was −6.08 to +1.54 returns per game. The 42-return training point and the two within-point freezes above establish that learning can help during a rally. They do not show reliable improvement across all further practice. [Matched held games](adjustment-24.json)

On the first 16 preassigned practice games, fitting a better movement-similarity metric on separate demonstrations reduced real returns from 151 to 121. A comparison trained to predict the relative outcome of two earlier physically scored alternatives on the same ball yielded 91. Reintroducing sixteen teacher-scored movement clips across the first 24 games produced 105 returns to the ordinary 179. Those mechanisms have not displaced the existing learner. The arm learned during individual rallies, but additional broad practice with this moving motor memory did not sustain transfer.
