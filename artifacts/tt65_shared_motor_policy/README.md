# Can a single directly optimized motor correction cover multiple mistakes?

A single conditional joint controller faced 24 attained practice situations across three equal groups: previously untouched low shots, touched misses and routine returns. Its fixed inputs came from an actual RGB-derived ball track, joint encoders and an existing archive of rewarded joint motions. Its output mixed three retrieved movements and smoothly adjusted each joint. The optimizer compared 32 parameter sets per generation on identical practice situations, including antithetic perturbations. Simulated legal returns supplied most of the objective, with smaller contact and approach signals. The practice plant received the attained native state. Assessment used one sensor-conditioned movement without candidate ranking, backpropagation or repeat attempts. The experiment borrowed the idea of normalized observations and repeated trials per policy perturbation from [Robotic Table Tennis with Model-Free Reinforcement Learning](https://www.columbia.edu/~wg2279/rtt/rtt.pdf).

After 15 generations (11,520 private practice rollouts) the best policy earned four simulated routine returns out of eight and no hard returns. On 15 release-disjoint, physical same-ball tests both initial and optimized policies missed every one of the ten prior misses. The initial pupil preserved three of five routine returns and the optimized one preserved two. This small shared model did not support a whole-game promotion.
