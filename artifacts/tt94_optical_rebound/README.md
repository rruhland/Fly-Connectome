# Reading an opponent's likely reply from the far-table bounce

Using two new post-bounce sightings and the preceding descent from ordinary stereo RGB, the arm can make a forecast a median of 158 ms before the opponent strikes. This anticipates a distribution of possible replies. The opposite paddle still sees the actual incoming stroke and independently samples its next goal. Of 128 held optical trajectories with a later measured reply, 123 gave a timely cue. Each individual forecast saw only its own past frames. The evaluator averages over independent physical redraws of a given outgoing stroke.

The first learner used 218 optical trials from 42 earlier outgoing contexts. On 22 held contexts its mean absolute error for the expected height was 24.0 mm. The same type of optical cue calibrated from 2,593 physical transitions across 64 separate release recordings brought this to 16.5 mm. A constant forecast gave 29.4 mm, and using only the descending arc gave 17.4 mm. On the broader fit the correlation between predicted and measured mean height was 0.83. This is advance information about a typical reply, with substantial remaining variability. Native motion is used to calibrate the broader teacher. The actor in a game receives camera measurements. The prospective game test uses that distribution to choose a preparatory joint reference from 2,596 actually rewarded cases on 65 previous releases. It balances travel toward attained movements at several lateral positions. As the actual incoming ball becomes visible, the ordinary direct joint controller takes over. Its 32 original-arm games use the TT80 prespecified draws.

In 32 full games, the proactive controller made 138 returns versus 135 for the ordinary continually learning arm. Median rally length changed from 3 to 3.5 and peak from 15 to 14. Ten games improved, ten shortened and twelve tied. On physically identical arrivals, 21 were returned by both, seven missed by both and two returns were lost. Across the course the learner made 168 forecasts and moved in preparation on 5,796 control ticks. A substantially better forecast of the *mean* next height was insufficient to make this particular preparation reliably beneficial.
