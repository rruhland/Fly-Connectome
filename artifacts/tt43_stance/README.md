# A single intermediate posture can reach new shots, but does not yet sustain broad rallies

The stock-clamp SO-101 has a larger practical range than the 38-return record's deep, negative-face motion suggests. A single intermediate position, reached during the preceding visible outbound flight, admits a clean shallow rescue on a low ball the stock stroke missed. On other balls that same position supports player-driven stereo returns at stock-like depth. The first optical pilot nevertheless shortened both ordinary rallies versus stock. Getting into the new posture is necessary but is not yet a reliable strike policy.

## Concentration and a conditional limit

The reference 38-return rally used a negative face every time. The three clean low rescues in its separate development course also admit negative-face solutions, but they are not close to the record's negative-face branch in actuator coordinates. Record leg 25 hit with wrist roll −1.504 rad, versus +2.641 rad for prior low rescue 4322. Their clocks from actual far separation were 612 and 365 ms. At 4.16 rad/s a single stationary joint reference cannot cover both of those archived strike positions even with zero delay after far separation. The best possible slack for those exact endpoints is −10 ms. After actual table separation the corresponding bound is −341 ms. This argument bounds reference travel to fixed archived joint positions. It does not bound every dynamically achievable contact on those arcs and does not treat the reference-rate bound as a limit on physical overshoot. ## Alternative contact geometry

The geometric screen used 38 actual ordinary incoming arcs from the record and three actual development lows. It sampled seven depths and three contact-normal/pace families on each independently propagated post-bounce flight. Both physical faces were tested. It admitted finite blade-center fits within a 71 mm tangential radius and then required collision-screened links, blade and table with 2 mm minimum clearance on the interpolated path. Actual clean witnessed poses were retained in the candidate sets. None of the geometry results alone claim a legal return.

The best sampled q is approximately [−0.45, −0.35, −0.28, +1.17, +0.55] rad at nominal grasp. Its routes reached all 41 rays in the travel screen from far separation and hit the negative face on 37 of 38 preferred ordinary geometric endpoints. Two of the three best low endpoints were negative and one positive. At a more conservative 3.45 rad/s proxy, the worst selected route has only 71 ms of slack after far separation, or −12 ms after an 83 ms observation delay. Even before acceleration and strike velocity are accounted for, proximity at the reference rate overstates what the plant can do on that shortest ordinary case. This is one readiness and a choice of strike after observing the incoming ball. It is not a second wing stance or a forced blade-face flip. The earliest inbound fits in the four archived clean low sensory examples appeared 58–77 ms after far separation. Their first visible bounce fits left only 73–104 ms before the low strokes. The numbers favor beginning to move on the incoming estimate and updating with the observed bounce, not waiting in a hub for bounce confirmation. ## Native feasibility and real optical play

An exact-state development coach moved to the common hub through physical bounded commands starting just after the prior near shot. No foreknowledge was used to change the far opponent, which delivered exactly the original far impact and near-table bounce. The body was within 0.0008 rad of the common hub at the chosen request tick, 83–100 ms after far separation. A 64-candidate, at most three-round local request used both signed poses and the unchanged rollout. On low 4322 the selected new stroke from that hub landed legally, unlike stock. The physical near collision was a positive-side rim hit at y = −1.053 m and z = 0.881 m, followed by a live opponent reply. The paired point rose from five to six physical arm landings. Independent tape replay reproduced every event and placement, with zero penetrations in 4,864 full CAD states and a 15.77 mm minimum distance. The same search missed the two chosen ordinary control cases. A shorter backswing on the original two-case roster yielded no usable native solution and is included as a negative. The successful physical correction is only an offline teacher example. The optical actor starts in the original boot state. Outbound stereo motion triggers the move to this same hub on each reception. From inbound RGB, its isolated predictor gets only stereo and own encoders/issued targets. It computes bounded early strike proposals around the predicted ray and optionally gives its normal-depth trajectory to the regular stock ensemble after a visible bounce. The ordinary fallback only replaces the early drive on its usual robust validation rule. Neither actor is told a shot ID, target, true bounce, or native ball state. All physical games use the stock clamp, motor envelope, paddle and same independent continuous opponent draw schedule.

The first frozen pilot used the first two direct-course assessment cards and the stock checkpoint after 12 live practice games. Pilot scores were 1 and 1 versus 5 and 10 stock. The optical policy reached within 0.02–0.06 rad of the new hub on recorded inbound cues in 9550 and returned its first ordinary ball on a new early negative-face movement. It also made a physical first return in the second game. One of its next nominally legal early plans on 9550 actually sent the ball back into its own half. The initial pilot commits a pre-bounce plan without adjusting it to the rebound, an avoidable limitation. In the follow-up on those identical cards, giving the deep trajectory back to the stock robust planner at observed rebound raised 9550 from 1 to 2 and left 15665 at 1. This does not preserve ordinary performance. A separate live practice card, 10329, started with the physically earned 4322 correction and made no legal arm return, so it did not update that correction bank. Exact independent action replay verified every referee event and original random target draw. The opening deliveries match stock within 1e−8 numerical tolerance. In all four pilot games, full CAD found zero penetrations across 7,610 states, with a 15.0 mm minimum clearance. These are small pilots, not a score-improving qualification. ## Low approaches with a camera-initialized actor

The cold fixed-hub actor was given the original continuous physical histories until just after the previous outbound collision on 4322 and 8302. The beginning of the intervention was scheduled for evaluation, but afterward the controller saw only RGB, own encoders and own issued commands. It reached the declared posture within 0.89 mrad and 7.38 mrad when it first saw the incoming balls. Both incoming events and bounces matched stock. It returned neither. On 4322, its first reliable inbound fit had no admissible geometry, and later it evaluated 160 low-hub proposals without a legal landing in its isolated model. On 8302 it never admitted a low-hub proposal. CAD found zero penetrations across 7,595 states. The gap to exact-state low 4322 is not an inability to reach the starting posture. [Matched camera forks](camera-low.json.gz)

A narrower low specialist tests whether uncertain early observations can still buy movement. It augments the camera fit with five fixed plausibly different vertical/longitudinal states, generates direct signed shallow strikes using each, and may seed their motor corrections with one physically scored example from a disjoint source. Before rebound, a proposal outside the nominal fit can only issue a temporary approach, not a full stroke. A refreshed fit must produce a nominal legal isolated rollout before a candidate commits. This is a motion-generation change, and preserves the ordinary policy when a viable normal-depth sequence already exists or the easy projected height is above 1.027 m. On release 8302, the added hypotheses changed the first optical search from zero admitted trajectories to seven modeled legal outcomes. None held up as the fit changed, and the external actor did not touch the actual ball. Across the attempt it tested 320 candidates. The same release-disjoint teacher and fixed deployment logic did not admit any timed candidate on 14843. Neither scheduled fork changed the far impact or near bounce, and replay reproduced the physical events in both. This is evidence that uncertain early geometry can buy an approach, not that the current camera model supports the whole strike. [Release-disjoint 4322 teacher transfer](camera-low-transfer.json.gz)
