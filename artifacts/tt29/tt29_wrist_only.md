# Wrist-only regulation rally

Moving the same 30 × 28 mm board by approximately 36 mm cleared the gripper's upcourt sightline from the lateral ready posture. The new aperture is 79 mm from the gripper frame origin. Two short standoffs support the rotated board. The direct table clamp and stock arm remain in use. The tested lens has a 102° vertical field of view and captures native 640 × 480 RGB. [Physical mount and controller](../../scripts/experimental/tt29_wrist_only.py)

The near controller obtained each flight estimate from the single wrist image and its capture-time arm encoders. It waited for multiple usable exposures and bounded the estimated depth uncertainty. The motor learner carried an existing bank of rewarded stereo-based actions acquired on the same measured opening family. During these trials the bank retrieved strokes from wrist observations and used legal landing reward to store new successful actions. The opposite end was the native-contact practice carriage. ## Verified continuous point

The refined controller required at least seven usable exposures. It rechecked the remaining stroke on fresh wrist evidence after the table bounce, with a 3 mm nominal clearance margin and a preference for centered blade contact. That version produced **22 alternating legal returns** on measured opening 7271, with 11 from the SO-101. Median departure speed was 5.49 m/s overall and 5.09 m/s from the near arm. The point lasted 16.4 simulated seconds. The action replay reproduced all 114 referee and contact events. Its full-CAD audit covered 7,874 physics states, including the initial and terminal states, with zero penetrations and a minimum checked clearance of 17.07 mm. Peak measured joint motion reached 6.11 rad/s, above the motor's 5.45 rad/s no-load reference. Stroke selection used physics search, and the trial took 461 wall-clock seconds on the shared machine. ## Matched solo calibration transfer

A separate clamped solo station used the same physical offset board. Rotating the wrist to use the opposite paddle face gave the camera a view of the falling ball. Twelve recorded command sequences were replayed on that station and produced 14 physical rebounds. The fit used 303 paired wrist and fixed-camera observations. Whole held-out drops contributed 78 more observations. On the held solo images the median reprojection error improved from 0.214 to 0.177 pixels. The fit moved the aperture estimate by 1.09 mm and rotated it by 0.153°. [Solo calibration](tt29_wrist_only_solo_flip.json)

Both regulation receivers used wrist RGB, encoders, the same carried motor memory, the same opening 7271 and the same opponent seed. One used the nominal mount drawing from a separate model. The other used the fit from the solo drops. The nominal receiver reached the 12-return cap. The solo-calibrated receiver completed six returns before its fourth near strike went out. Both trials had zero full-CAD penetrations. The improved solo image fit yielded a shorter rally in this matched pair. [Matched continuous trials](tt29_wrist_only_warm.json.gz)

## Local wrist correction on hard openings

The opt-in variant used the same native wrist RGB, encoders, offset mount and starting motor bank as its control. When a fresh wrist forecast shifted the projected contact, it tested six small changes to the joint targets alongside the original commands in an independent dynamics model. Both conditions enabled the signed motor speed–torque envelope in the simulated arm and model. The score was capped at the first legal near-side return. [Experiment](../../scripts/experimental/tt29_wrist_local.py)

With full stroke search enabled, both conditions returned measured opening 7345 legally. The local check applied one correction about 171 ms of simulated time before impact. The actual first far bounce moved 22.8 mm inward from the sideline and 44.0 mm deeper. Departure speed rose from 4.06 to 4.17 m/s. Peak measured joint rate rose from 3.40 to 3.59 rad/s, below the modeled 5.45 rad/s no-load reference. [7345 paired trial](tt29_wrist_local.json.gz)

With search limited to recalled strokes and local adjustments, both conditions scored zero on each of the three completed held pairs (11292, 4527 and 13817). Two retargets on 4527 passed the model check, and the live stroke still bounced in the near half. The model found no viable local candidate for the other two openings. Five local checks took a median of 261 ms in wall time on the shared machine, compared with a 16.7 ms control tick. The proposed gaze pulse never activated in the completed pairs. The interrupted fourth pair (6703) is excluded. All eight completed plays together had zero penetrations across 4,472 full-CAD states. [Held paired trials](tt29_wrist_local_held.json.gz)
