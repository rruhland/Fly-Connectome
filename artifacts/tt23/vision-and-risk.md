# Does a better forecast of a swing produce a better actual return?

The SO-101 predicted what would happen after each of its recorded motor commands, then compared those predictions with subsequent stereo images. The training set contained 166 strokes from 51 distinct earlier points. It included swings that missed. No simulator contact, position or spin label entered this learning step. The nominal simulator and the stereo court registration were supplied. [Practice motions and errors](registration-practice.json.gz) · [Fitter](../../scripts/experimental/tt23_optical_contact.py)

The best single camera offset fitted on other points increased mean capped camera error from 69.9 to 84.9 mm when a whole donor point was held out. A learned mixture of 45 small offsets predicted the distribution of observed errors better. Its donor-held mean negative log score was 1.562, compared with 1.659 for uniform weights and 1.998 for the single nominal forecast. These scores use a fixed 35 mm error scale. [Distribution and weights](risk-weights.json.gz)

On six fresh, ordinary near serves, each model had the same seven-example optical memory, receiving partner, stroke library and camera input. The new ranker used nine representative offsets to select a stroke. The original controller and the same nine offsets with uniform weights produced rallies of 0, 0, 0, 9, 5 and 25. Learned weights produced 0, 0, 0, 7, 5 and 15. The learner made 12 legal returns versus 18 under either control. All 18 two-arm command tapes reproduced the physical outcomes. Keep the original ranking. The learned visual error distribution was a better predictor on archived motor actions and a worse guide to choosing new actions. ## Where the forecast error originates

A separate evaluation replayed all 11 points of the original ordinary game. At 36 actual stroke decisions it held the issued motor commands fixed and replaced different parts of the camera estimate with their simulator values **only inside a diagnostic forecaster**. Mean position error at about six frames after the nominal interception was 62.8 mm with camera input, 51.6 mm with exact spin alone, 52.0 mm with exact velocity alone and 58.4 mm with exact position alone. Supplying all three reduced the error to 20.9 mm. For 20 of the 36 strokes the ordinary inverse-flight fit had been unavailable; on those the median absolute spin error around the lateral axis was 56.1 rad/s. The camera estimates, fitted physics and stroke timing all have room to improve.
