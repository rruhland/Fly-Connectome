# Independent time-to-ten confirmation

Eight fresh training streams used the exact same policy, prior strength, experienced feedback and learned physical opponent as the initial four runs. Each learner played up to twelve points, stopping after its first ten-return rally. Outcomes include the same first full point when both learners chose the same action. All 92 recorded policy episodes passed strict command replay. [Preregistered schedules and censoring](online-rally-confirm-design.md) · [Initial four complete twelve-point streams](online-rally.md)

| 7 | 8 (21.6 s) | 8 (17.3 s) |
| 8 | 4 (10.4 s) | 4 (10.4 s) |

The Pong prior produced a ten-return point in all eight streams. The flat prior reached it in six. In all six common successes both did it on the same point. Flat finished the qualifying exchange earlier on three streams, Pong earlier on one, with two ties. The two extra source successes took 17.1 and 17.6 seconds; the flat learners exhausted their twelve points at 20.0 and 24.9 seconds. At the predeclared joint budget of twelve points and 40 seconds, success was 8/8 versus 6/8. These two cold failures are censored before the full 40 seconds. For point exposure, two favorable differences and six ties give a one-sided exact sign probability of .25. The earlier saving of several points therefore did not recur on the six streams both conditions solved.

Across the initial and confirmatory cohorts, each learner could receive point-duration feedback from only its selected opening. The eight independent results support an increase in the observed success frequency with the given physical tools. They do not establish a large population-wide decrease in time to ten.
