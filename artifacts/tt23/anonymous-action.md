# Learning where to move without an interception target

In native Pong the learner could choose seven fixed paddle displacements. It never received a formula for where the ball would meet the paddle. It estimated action direction from its own motor probes and used a previously learned visual-role model to describe a moving region relative to its resting paddle. In 72 training starts, the target for each displacement was simply the elapsed point time. A kernel regression estimated which motion would prolong play. The only imposed directional symmetry was that reflecting a scene and a motor displacement together preserves their value. An independent shuffled model received the same observations and displacements with the durations permuted within each source start.

On 36 other native Pong starts the trained paddle made 13 first returns versus 9 with shuffled experience and 6 holding its initial position. Mean survival was 3.42, 3.13 and 2.57 seconds respectively. [Native source and held trials](anonymous-action-source.json.gz)

The SO-101 then chose from the same fractions of the workspace. Eight visual observations supplied a moving region's position and velocity, and the robot's own motor reference supplied its resting pose. These inputs went into the frozen value model. A previously practiced timed swing executed the selected physical displacement. The scored point had no simulated contact search in the opening policy.

On 64 fresh arm starts, the source model made 52 actual contacts and 17 legal returns. Holding position made 25 and 11. The prespecified shuffled model did better than both, making 63 and 21. Because all seven physical actions were scored on each launch, a further 1,024 within-start source-label permutations could be assessed exactly on those same launches. They averaged 18.6 contacts and 5.9 legal returns, with 13% matching or exceeding the source model's count on each outcome. A directionally favorable policy can arise by chance. Independent source fits and new target launches were reserved to measure whether reward training reliably improves the direction. Four independent 48-start Pong training datasets then tested the same fixed learner on 64 additional arm launches. Legal returns were 2, 23, 0 and 0 out of 64. Their paired, source-label-permuted learners scored 18, 0, 0 and 18. Pooling all source experience gave zero legal returns and 17 contacts; keeping the motor centered gave 18 and 22. Against 1,024 permutations the mean legal-return result across fits had a one-sided permutation p of .497. The arm's visual contexts lay about 2.4 kernel widths from the nearest example in the first Pong fit, and each source fit tended to choose one movement magnitude on both sides. The pooled policy failed the opening criterion for a full-rally test. This demonstrates why a single successful shuffled or trained source should not be used as evidence for this kind of transfer.
