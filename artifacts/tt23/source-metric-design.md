# Do Pong outcomes help the arm reuse a later stroke?

The transfer intervention learns a one-dimensional motion coordinate from native Pong return feedback. The coordinates come from the visible mover and the previously identified actor before contact. They describe transverse offset, incoming slope and projected transverse miss relative to the approach distance, plus the time to the actor plane in camera frames. The source event score supplies a binary return label. A ridge fit to training Pong rallies defines the coordinate. Entire native launch seeds are held out to measure how well that coordinate carries native return outcomes.

The arm uses the existing SO-101 motor proposals unchanged. Every learner shares the target-trained opening head and the fixed lateral correction used to collect the physical practice branches. At each later cue the learner can choose a depth, face angle, pace and lead. An observed legal return or failure updates a weighted Beta estimate of target reward on nearby motor actions and optical contexts. The Pong coordinate only changes which target observations count as similar. Compare its effect with the common metric alone and with a feature learned from permuted Pong return labels. The two augmented metrics use the same source normalization and weight. Pong does not nominate a stroke or provide an arm reward.

## Target practice and evaluation

Give each learner one selected physical outcome at each practice cue. Replay the same cue orders and per-action exploration uniforms across all three conditions. Exclude all cues from the complete held donor point, including its opening outcome, before estimating the arm prior or selecting practice actions. Assess the physical outcome at each held cue after 0, 4, 8, 16, 24 and 32 practice trials. The primary contrast is the mean held strict legal-return difference over the 4-, 8- and 16-trial budgets. Resample entire held donors for paired intervals. Take the fixed random practice order on the full donor pool, freeze after 16 outcomes, and play all three policies on six fresh physical points. Keep the shared first move through the first second-leg cue. Record alternating returns, second and later near returns, 10/20-return counts and full two-arm replay.

Each practice opportunity is a branch from archived native geometry. Cue reset, access to eligible arms, and restriction to already visited contexts apply to this archive comparison. A fresh physical match has no state restores or unchosen-trial feedback.
