# Does learning to mirror a motion make it easier to cover the opposite wing?

The body-only calibration discovered sign reversals for shoulder pan and wrist roll. Across 64 held joint configurations the median reflected blade-center error was 2.95 mm and the 90th percentile was 6.89 mm. The learner used this map to propose joint movements from previously scored movements on the other wing, then screened their physical effects in its camera-initialized model. [Body calibration](calibration.json)

Across the same eight frozen-memory games, combining ordinary joint movements produced 89 legal returns. Adding the reflected proposals produced 44. Five games changed at the first intervention. In all five, both arms returned exactly the same incoming ball, but the resulting rallies tended to end sooner. An alternative interpolator selected six mixtures between joint schedules with opposing errors and made 42. A reliability screen based on physically observed landing rates also made 42. These experiments produced new legal movements but did not improve sustained play. The comparison of selecting versus combining learned actions was motivated in part by Muelling and colleagues' cooperative table-tennis work. The experiments here mixed bounded direct joint movements and measured real returns under the same opponent draw.
