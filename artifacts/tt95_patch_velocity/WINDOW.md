The body-screened condition tests potential changes with a body-only dynamics check with no ball or opponent. Its initial late-clock nominal command survived screening unchanged in both cases. The no-ball patch models were not used as proof of the score. The real native prefix, far contact, near bounce and physical referee are held fixed, and every motion-window legal answer reproduced its exact event digest. The 27.5 mm projected-normal window sent it at 3.08 m/s and earned a reply. The late-clock initial full program retained by a native body screen also made a real return at 3.17 m/s. This separates geometric contact, useful signed impulse and awareness of trajectory phase. Adding tangent is neither necessary nor sufficient on these cases. The two 7398 answers were near the rim and do not demonstrate a new broad success.

## Public forecasts on two matched native low shots

A separate actor derived its contact clock from ordinary RGB and the velocities/normals from scored encoder history rather than the original successful motion. Terminal and contact-window policies each issued a novel candidate that was nominally legal in the public twin on both 14429 and 15541. All four actual matched balls still failed. All CAD paths were clean.

At 14429, the terminal proposal clipped the rim and ultimately struck a fixture. The window proposal sent the actual ball forward and upward, instead of backwards and down, and changed the terminal fault to an own-half bounce. Its contact remained near the outer blade edge at 75.1 mm, not the 27.5 mm broad mechanical witness. Both 15541 proposals contacted near the rim and sent the ball downward. Better generic attainment did not transfer a hard-return skill through the live registration and strike selection problem. [Public matched motion windows](window-public.json.gz)

## Equal-clock control

The equal-clock single-terminal condition separates using the later aim time from tracking multiple points. On 14429 the later projected-normal terminal condition reproduced the **exact same commands and score** as the projected-normal window. On 7398 the later projected-normal terminal still missed, while the projected-normal window produced an answer. A later full-velocity terminal legally landed on 7398, but the opponent did not return it. Thus the later clock alone explains the main 14429 recovery, and the multi-sample objective mattered to the chosen motion on 7398. Neither comparison shows that adding tangent itself improves legal answering. [Equal-clock stroke control](same-clock-terminal.json.gz)

## Receding public correction

The two previously issued public contact-window paths were replayed exactly until a new decision. At five later stereo windows, the actor compared continuing that same tape with generating a new patch-window proposal from the closer observation. The newer estimates made the original path nonlegal in all five nominal checks. The new proposals found two nominal legal paths, and separate stress placements sometimes found legal paths, but no single replacement passed both criteria. The real commands and event tapes therefore stayed identical. This shows a failure to replace an increasingly implausible plan, not a successful real improvement or proof that late correction cannot help.

The TT84 diagnostic labels 14429 and 7398 are in that study's hard low class. With the stricter projected-height threshold of 1.005 m, 14429 and 7398 are above it. The stricter 15541 case, projected to 0.998 m, is the public near-rim failure.
