# TT87 | Re-aiming the stock wrist board opens its cone, but mostly not its sightline

The supported 55° optical re-aim changed no ball, paddle, or joint in the paired pose replays. With the same 124° lens and original aperture, 36 of 37 held deadline frames geometrically contained the incoming ball, versus 0 of 37 at the stock aim. Nonetheless, only one of ten distinct held deliveries yielded the required two unclipped natural RGB ball sightings with the blade visible. No untouched central low ball gained that cue. This is a changed-sensor feasibility result, not an improvement to the original-camera 100-return benchmark.

The useful case was an unscored touch on 9230. Its re-aimed camera first saw the incoming ball 196 ms before physical impact and delivered three unclipped exposures inside the 67–125 ms decision window. Its deadline bearing errors had a 1.30 mm median. One routine scored control, 9254, obtained one verified unclipped deadline image. The stock replay had no useful overlap on any of the ten.

The limiting obstruction was the original bracket, not the PCB's new angle or the RGB detector. At each of 92 decision frames the evaluator traced nine physical rays from the actual aperture toward the ball's center and interior silhouette. The stock bracket blocked all nine through 50/55 training frames and 32/37 held frames. Excluding the entire rotated PCB, barrel and screws from the evaluator rays did not open even one additional ray. That establishes the sampled obstruction independently of how the lens is aimed. The aperture must be repositioned or its opaque bracket changed to open those rays, or the preceding arm posture must change. None of those was part of this test.

## Mechanical and optical comparison

- The optical search used 55 actual moving-wrist, postbounce frames from sixteen TT85 releases, never the held trajectories. It searched tilt, azimuth, and roll using the actual calibrated aperture and encoder chain. The objective required a projected ball, blade, and jaw in the fixed 124° field of view before the decision deadline.
- In the lattice, 926 orientations geometrically opened two or more difficult training shots. Forty-two retained the tested stock bracket and housing clearance across the moving wrist samples. The selected 55° tilt fits 43/55 training frames and supplies at least two geometrically valid images in 13/16 training shots. The measured board-to-bracket interface distance was −1.19 mm across those frames. The original modeled attachment already overlaps by −3.95 mm. The diagnostic allowed no new enclosure intrusion beyond 0.5 mm on the checked surfaces, retained the physical mounting overlap, and fixed the aperture position at (+6,+54,−35) mm.
- The initial broader installation pilot is separately preserved. Its top optic had a +2.14 mm unsupported board-to-bracket gap and was excluded mechanically. Its stopped preview capture was not reviewed or used to choose the final camera.
- To isolate optical effects, the altered full opaque lens housing, sensor, board and screws were rotated together for each render, then restored before the next physics tick. The existing bracket, jaw, gripper, paddle and sensors stayed opaque. This reproduced the actual moving physical arm, far hit, bounce and near-contact packet exactly in both views. The optic fit used no learned motor action or hidden game input. The stock physical game system was not altered.

The present fixed-aperture mount is therefore too unreliable for a generic wrist correction. No fusion or whole-game action modifications were attempted.
