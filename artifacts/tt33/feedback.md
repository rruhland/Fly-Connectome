# Playing from direct joint outputs

A frozen SO-101 control policy trained on 30 previously successful strikes landed three successive shots on its left, right and left in a seven-return rally. Its controller updated the arm’s joint commands at 60 Hz from stereo ball measurements at 30 Hz. It used a learned regression from observations to joint setpoints and an engineered estimate of the approaching ball at one fixed depth. The robot executed no stroke searches or stored command sequences during play. The three arm contacts span about 52 cm across the shoulder. Full CAD replay reproduced the point with zero penetrations across 2,999 states. ## Can feedback change an outcome?

We kept the eight held measured launch IDs and changed the opponent seeds before any controller was run on those variants. On this test one opening accounted for all three legal arm returns. Reissuing the direct controller’s earlier motor recordings without new camera feedback produced zero legal returns on the changed points. Those same recordings reproduced the referee events exactly on all eight original seeds. All six previously landed arm returns therefore provide a positive replay control. The changed-seed test uses the same releases, a different incoming distribution, and a small sample. [Changed games](changed-direct.json.gz) · [Blind control](changed-blind.json) · [Controller implementation](../../scripts/experimental/tt33_joint.py)

We also changed only the opponent’s lateral request **after** the SO-101's first return, while retaining its original depth, height and pace requests for the second delivery. All eight opening strokes and first-bounce outcomes reproduced exactly. Four points reached the changed reply. Reissuing the unchanged recordings then returned none of the next four balls. Updating joint commands from camera feedback returned one of those four. For that point, 10485, the original controller had previously returned only the opening on the unmodified schedule. This is evidence that sensor feedback can alter a later shot. Most unexpected replies still ended the rally. [Matched mid-point intervention and both controls](reply-change.json.gz)

The raw-ball regression still detects an incoming ball and uses a shared rest configuration. Training used calibrated stereo and previously rewarded teacher frames. The plane controller matched the wider teacher on these eight points. On the earlier eight varied points, it landed two arm returns versus the wider teacher’s eight. On the fresh course it missed both centrally placed openings. Corrective teaching on two practice games followed by removing the teacher preserved six fresh legal returns. The first three pupil variants' 24 fresh games all reproduced their referee events on command-only playback with zero CAD penetrations. ## A direct controller with fewer task assumptions

We also fitted a local joint controller on 3,138 sensor-action examples throughout complete earlier demonstrations. Its input contained the current optically estimated ball position and velocity, five measured joint angles and joint velocities. Its output was a new actuator setpoint at every tick with usable optical evidence. It retained the ordinary colored-ball detector, stereo calibration and an optical flight estimator. It had no incoming-direction gate, receiving plane, contact countdown, stroke type or return-to-home instruction. In the absence of recent vision it issued zero increments. Complete teacher games supplied the examples, including recoveries and misses. [Feedback implementation](../../scripts/experimental/tt33_local.py) · [Frozen sensor-action model](local-model.npz)

On the eight earlier varied points this policy landed one legal return. On the eight fresh points it landed two, both in a single uninterrupted five-return rally. The full CAD replay measured the physical contacts. This is a direct sensory-to-joint-control baseline with a narrower repertoire than its teacher. [Watch the five-return point](local-8239.mp4) · [Physical replay](local-8239.json) · [Fresh games](local-fresh.json.gz)

In 48 subsequent practice games this same controller added small exploratory joint offsets. It reinforced recent sensor-action samples after a real earned return, skipping the opening feed. The learner kept operating as it updated. It landed 14 own returns and retained 824 new samples. Frozen afterward, its earlier varied score remained one, while its fresh score fell from two to one. A separate simpler controller that learned which of nine joint offsets to use from 55 observed approach outcomes landed seven training returns over 48 points, and zero in both held cohorts. A fixed global joint offset chosen by paired physical reward trials yielded one fresh return. These controls show why successful practice actions alone make a poor training set for correcting misses. ## Research direction

Use the stroke planner to generate initial demonstrations and occasional corrective labels on unfamiliar states the direct controller actually visits. Keep it off throughout evaluation. Train the general body-control layer on how joint actions alter the next visual and encoder observations, including during misses, and keep the policy head that chooses actions for task reward separate. Measure learning on the first few unfamiliar replies, replies after the opening, and retention on earlier points with the same checkpoint. Training on states visited by the student addresses the distribution shift between demonstrating a strike and recovering from the student's own movement. Body transfer, wrist-only control and autonomous improvement in the direct pupil remain to be demonstrated. [DAgger and sequential imitation learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html)
