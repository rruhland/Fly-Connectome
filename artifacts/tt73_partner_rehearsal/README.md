# Can a responsive practice partner deliver the challenge the learner needs?

The practice partner makes up to four physical previews of continuously sampled goals, each from the current opponent state. It chooses among them using the student’s recent success rate within receiving regions. On 35% of decisions it keeps the ordinary unaltered random goal. The student receives only the actual RGB, encoders and ordinary legal reward from one point. Frozen evaluation uses the original fully random physical partner on both native fixtures. Extra partner computation is charged to the coach. [Practice protocol](course/course.json)

In the pilot, the coach previewed 110 target offers over 41 decisions and measured 39 delivered crossings in both the hypothetical and actual worlds. Mean absolute differences were 0.21 mm laterally and 4.19 mm in projected height. The largest height error was 82.5 mm. All 39 received offers counted, including those beyond the 32-offer threshold needed to finish the final point. The pilot establishes enough physical fidelity to try practice. It does not establish a change in the learner. [All pilot observations](pilot/accuracy.json)

## What the arm retained

After ordinary random practice on the clamp shifted 12 cm, the frozen learner moved back to the centered arm and made **175 returns across 16 held games**, up from 73 before the extra practice. Median rally length rose from five to 9.5. Five games reached 15 returns, the longest reached 31, and both checkpoints recorded three zeros. Nine games improved, four shortened and three tied. The release bootstrap interval for the mean gain was +1.69 to +11.38 returns per game. On 17 physically identical incoming balls the two checkpoints landed the same 14 and missed the same three. Much of the difference came after different successful strokes changed what the opponent sent back. The comparison includes both extra practice and the change of body. It does not isolate a special advantage of changing bodies. [Held assessment and paired arrivals](course/results.json) · [Centered game tapes after practice](course/uniform-original.json.gz)

On the shifted arm itself, the same frozen checkpoint made 73 returns compared with 46 before practice, with medians five and two. The preview-based teaching alternative gave 55 on the shifted arm and 57 on return to center. Ordinary practice cost 696 CPU seconds for 275 delivered balls in 49 points. The coach used 941 CPU seconds for 260 in 54 points. In each condition the final point was allowed to finish. The coach forecast all 260 physically delivered arrivals, to a mean absolute error of 0.14 mm laterally and 5.18 mm in projected height, without producing a stronger student.
