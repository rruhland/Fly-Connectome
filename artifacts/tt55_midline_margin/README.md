# The forecourt blade interior was reachable, but the face gave the wrong rebound

On the same central-low 4214 offer that could be returned off the rim, both paddle faces reached clearly interior contact on the unchanged stock body. The obstacle was not getting the ball onto the blade. It was getting an appropriate face orientation and outgoing velocity at that contact.

The joint-space route scan searched 14 depths, both signed faces, three incoming-to-outgoing impulse settings, five tilt and yaw combinations and multiple initial poses. It found 18 routed candidates. The closest forecourt fits at y = −1.00 to −1.13 m had nominal planar-radius errors under 8 mm. A second family fit near y = −1.69 m. In the intermediate −1.27 to −1.62 m interval this particular fit did not find any route meeting the recorded position, face and clearance thresholds. This is a finite search, not a proof of physical exclusion. [Fixed geometry plan](plan.json)

Six actual physically attained approach paths preserved the archived incoming event prefix. A bounded moving-strike search ran 2,592 isolated native trials across them, including both forecourt and deep signed faces. Thirteen were modeled legal, all on one forecourt route near y = −1.07 m and all at a radius of at least 75 mm. No modeled strike met the registered 58 mm interior threshold, so no interior candidate was issued from this sweep. [Signed body routes and native trials](result.json.gz)

A second physical assay settled all 18 poses eight control ticks before the earlier handoff, then held the actual signed reference. All 18 matched the same far strike and near bounce. Seventeen made native contacts, and 15 contacts were within 58 mm of the blade center. The tightest measured radial error was 9.17 mm. No strike landed legally. At the available closest forecourt contacts the wrist flex was near its 1.658 rad joint position limit and the face axis was steep. A representative center contact had a native contact normal aligned with the flat paddle surface, and then sent the ball at −.32 m/s along the forward axis and +3.59 m/s vertically. Both face signs and the deeper family show the same general backward-and-upward failure for the passive receiving geometry. [All exact reevaluated physical controls](settle.json.gz) · [Independent native radius measurement](contact-geometry.json)

This narrows the problem to the orientation and outgoing impulse supported by the particular branch, not simple reach or missing finalist slots. No new controller or qualification score is claimed.

## A flatter face and a different active drive

Relaxing the exact-center requirement to a bounded interior disk found 35 possible early routes. The lowest modeled vertical normal component found in the physically useful forward depths was about .51. Further tilted solutions no longer met the simultaneous signed-plane, radial and preparation constraints. Twelve distinct flatter poses were physically settled on the original intact offer. All touched, seven at measured radii within 58 mm, and none scored. [Flatter-pose plan](flat-protocol.json) · [Physical contacts and route fits](flat.json.gz)

A separate active family changed the earlier body route to a backswing and then swept forward and down along the steep face, not simply harder in its normal direction. It tested one positive face, one reverse face and the different deep posture. All nine attainable preparatory branches matched the original actual incoming prefix. Of 432 native trajectory candidates, 327 made predicted interior contacts and just six were modeled legal. All six were outside the registered interior region. Because none cleared the interior condition, no new physical claimant, neighboring hold or optical policy was promoted. These finite tests rule out the sampled approaches as a useful interior control family, not every possible stock motion.
