# Do optical misses teach a useful change to joint movement?

At the end of a failed attempt the learner estimated the closest visible ball-to-paddle displacement from stereo ball motion, joint encoders and its calibrated body model. When it could see a change in ball direction, it also measured the direction of that impulse. It compared these effects with its own scored attempts in similar contexts, converted the difference into a bounded joint change and applied that change early on later incoming balls. This required no future simulator state or backpropagation. [Implementation](../../scripts/experimental/tt45_error_learning.py)

Across the 24 continuous random games, the readout earned 124 returns on 146 deliveries. The same temporal motor feedback with no error update earned 141 on 162. Both peaked at 24 and the median fell from 3.5 to three. Five games improved and ten shortened. The learner recorded 124 successes, 17 spatial miss corrections and five departure corrections. The negative result concerns applying the estimated correction to the early movement. The real contact error can also depend on much later commands.
