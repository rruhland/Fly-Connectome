# Does more initial practice make the next 96 games easier?

The younger learner, starting after 12 practice games, made 569 legal returns during the next 96 games. The learner starting after 48 practice games made 483. Both updated during every game. All 32 opening recordings were different from the original training releases. Each block revisited them in a new order with new independent opening and opponent seeds. | Starting motor memory | First 32 | Middle 32 | Last 32 | Median over 96 | Games reaching 15 | Best |

| After 12 practice games | 164 | 198 | 207 | 3 | 14 | 25 |
| After 48 practice games | 196 | 156 | 131 | 2 | 11 | 42 |

The younger learner's median rose from two to three to 3.5. Its final block had eight zero-return points. For the older learner the corresponding medians were 2.5, four and two. The release-cluster bootstrap interval for the younger learner's advantage runs from −0.67 to +2.44 returns per game. The streams changed between blocks. A before/after comparison on independent opening recordings is needed to distinguish what the young learner retained from variation in the balls it received.
