# SO-101 rally and Pong transfer benchmark

The primary endpoint is a continuous exchange with at least ten legal returns after the serve. Each side must land at least five. Also report exchanges reaching twenty, both sides' totals, all point lengths, and timeouts. Every counted return must reach the other half following a physical paddle contact under the existing strict referee. One serve starts each point. There are no subsequent releases, state restores or discarded misses. An opponent that misses before returning contributes a short point.

For an independent test, use all twelve opponent-served starts from NumPy `default_rng(220381117).integers(2**31, size=12)` in order. Freeze the proposed policy and the feed distribution before using these starts. At least three ten-return points on that set is the first repeatability target for cooperative practice. Report a narrower opening distribution, fixed landing lane, stereo calibration, model-based planning, or coaching explicitly. Hold competitive games, variable lanes and single-camera runs out as distinct conditions.

A controlled robot can demonstrate physics feasibility. Evidence for **learning to rally** requires an improving outcome from earlier experience on new starts relative to the same initial policy. Evidence for **Pong speeding arm learning** requires equal target practice and learner code, source-trained versus cold, preferably a scrambled-source control, and differences in target legal returns or points of practice to threshold. A better video prediction alone cannot establish this. A fixed collision objective, CAD, or known semantic tracking supplied by the implementation must be disclosed when interpreting the result.
