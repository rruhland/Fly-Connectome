# Receiving policies that failed to extend rallies consistently

A ranker trained from actual legal credit for proposed strokes lengthened a five-start development set from 2, 4, 0, 4, 6 to 2, 4, 0, 6, 12. The same ranker then reduced the frequency of ten-return rallies from two to one across twelve wider held starts with the learned five-axis partner. A spin and velocity correction fitted against previous observed outgoing shots reached 2, 4, 0, 4, 9 on the development set. [Stroke success ranker](point-feedback-pilot.json.gz) · [Five-axis comparison](paired-course.json.gz) · [Optical calibration](identification-pilot.json.gz)

Retrieving motor recipes from prior scored optical shots while omitting candidate collision simulation yielded one two-return exchange in ten starts. A random recipe control yielded two. A short sequence imitator that retrieved joint targets from raw visual features made seven legal near-arm returns in eight starts. Its blind version made nine on those same starts. A 12-proposal physics search using successful motor recipes reached 4, 4, 4, 2, 10 alternating returns in five points. A random library of equal size reached 8, 4, 6, 7, 6. None of these comparisons justifies promoting the prior recipe library as the default motor policy. ## Paddle-edge contact

The command-driven optical shadow predicted physical paddle contact on 134 of 135 archived swings. Of the 135 real swings, 107 returned legally. Median distance from the center of the predicted impact, divided by paddle radius, was 0.379 for legal returns and 0.386 for faults. A fit using local impact geometry reached a seed-held-out AUC of 0.673, where 0.5 is chance. These data give little reason to prioritize a center-of-paddle penalty as the main fix for the short rallies. [Observations and fitted score](face-practice.json.gz)
