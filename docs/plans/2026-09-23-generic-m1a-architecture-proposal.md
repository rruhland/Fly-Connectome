# Proposed generic M1A visual architecture

**Status:** Experimental proposal only. Do not change production M1A without user approval. This supersedes the withdrawn Pong-specific motion/world-state proposal.

## Objective and boundaries

M1A should convert ON/OFF event-camera evidence into a useful representation of coherent visual patterns and their likely future activity. It should work independent of the evaluation environment's object names or physics. Do not encode ball or paddle identity, image-column crops, walls, collision classes, intercepts, simulator state, or any game-specific target in M1A. Pong may be one held-out evaluation environment. M1B remains motor learning; a fixed controller can later test whether M1A forecasts are actionable.

Use no backpropagation. Preserve the measured connectome graph and transmitter signs as a biological reference; mark additional computational motifs explicitly rather than claiming they are measured synapses. Prior opt-in T5c/d activity shows that the graph can supply motion-selective signals, but subtype-rate direction was insufficient for small dots. The opt-in spatial event map and local displacement competition show stronger generic translation transfer across shape, position, contrast, and two speeds. Their learned scalar efficacy did **not** beat a matched fixed control.

## Candidate information flow

1. **Sensory access.** Keep signed ON/OFF events, local decaying sensory memory, and measured T4/T5 responses available. Fixed local event correlations may serve as low-level sensory primitives and explicit controls. They must **not** define the final motion code, object state, or prediction rule.
2. **Learned recurrent visual state.** Repeated local microcircuits receive raw sensory activity through initially uncommitted local synapses. Sparse competition and local Hebbian/homeostatic plasticity can form visual feature selectivity from experience; recurrent eligibility and each cell's predictive mismatch can learn temporal dependencies. The latent units should be free to encode whatever local visual structure improves prediction, including motion, persistence, texture, or interactions. No neuron is assigned an ON/OFF motion direction, object identity, speed, or game role by the implementation.
3. **Coherence and memory.** Interactions among neighboring latent units should support visual continuity, multiple simultaneous patterns, and uncertainty through brief missing evidence. The [generic grouping baseline](../experiments/2026-09-23-generic-grouping-findings.md) is a diagnostic ceiling only. Its explicit centroids, displacement estimates, and raw event integrator should not become M1A's learned representation. The noise failure shows that recurrent memory needs confidence or renewed sensory evidence.
4. **Predictive readout.** Decode likely future sensory activity or latent activity at several camera-frame horizons from the learned recurrent state. Delayed observations update local synapses using local eligibility and prediction error, without backpropagation. A matched frozen recurrent circuit, shuffled-time control, and fixed-correlation/extrapolation control are required. Learning must improve prediction and latent transfer beyond these controls before promotion.

The learned recurrent population is the central M1A representation. Correlation filters and explicit trackers can help test what information the sensory stream contains, but cannot substitute for evidence that recurrent plasticity discovers reusable structure. This order avoids forcing early L1/L2/L3 cells to supervise their own future activation or assigning direction/velocity labels to latent channels.

The [first learned-latent tests](../experiments/2026-09-23-learned-latent-homeostasis-findings.md) provide a narrow positive sensory-code result but expose a recurrent-learning failure. Homeostatic local Hebbian coding improved held-out direction readability, while a feedforward-selected code plus separate recurrent predictor failed to fit even training trajectories well. The next candidate must let recurrent activity influence the current inferred state and must distinguish conflicting local temporal contexts in its eligibility/credit; do not promote the current dictionary or recurrent rule as a working M1A representation.

The [learned transition-unit probe](../experiments/2026-09-24-learned-transition-units-findings.md) offers a more concrete foothold: competitive local units learned ordered sensory-code patterns that transferred across held-out shapes, and their predictive synapses beat shuffled credit at both tested speeds. Forecast accuracy remained low. A [matched empirical readout](../experiments/2026-09-24-empirical-transition-readout-findings.md) improved next-latent prediction but still fit future visual events poorly. The next capacity test must hold these learned codes fixed and ask whether a stronger evaluation-only local-field decoder can extract the future. This separates state sufficiency from plasticity/readout design before another production architecture proposal.

The [frozen-code capacity test](../experiments/2026-09-24-frozen-code-capacity-findings.md) found that a local linear readout can fit square training events while failing to transfer across shapes; every target event is spatially reachable. The next experiment should broaden **unlabeled experience** rather than assign motion channels or game objects. Train the same local plastic circuit on varied connected moving patterns and hold new patterns out, then remeasure both learned prediction and the frozen-code decoder ceiling. This will show whether data diversity can yield reusable state before redesigning the local predictive update.

The [equal-budget diverse-experience test](../experiments/2026-09-24-diverse-visual-experience-findings.md) raised held-out decoder F1 only from 0.296 to 0.324 at the registered threshold and hurt slow-motion transfer. The varied arm also underfit its own training scenes, so check one larger exposure budget with the same circuit and held-out set. If that fails, stop tuning this narrow code and test a different local temporal-state mechanism.

The [fourfold-exposure check](../experiments/2026-09-24-expanded-visual-exposure-findings.md) failed to improve prediction despite better post-hoc direction readability. Stop scaling the event-site transition code. The next opt-in candidate should give learned latent units a different local temporal state, then require improved future-event prediction across shape and speed before any production revision.

The [correlation-input latent experiment](../experiments/2026-09-24-correlation-input-latent-findings.md) is the first strong transfer result: an unlabeled learned code over fixed low-level event coincidences achieved 47/48 post-hoc direction readability and 0.669 held-out next-event F1, far above matched random and shuffled-credit controls at both speeds. Its frozen-code offline decoder reached 0.918 F1. Preserve this candidate and test interrupted and multi-pattern motion next. The predictor does not yet feed back into latent inference, and the fixed sensory control still wins on simple constant translation, so do not promote it as working generic M1A yet.

The [frozen robustness suite](../experiments/2026-09-24-correlation-latent-robustness-findings.md) preserved learned prediction on two independent movers and rate changes, with weaker but still positive transfer through crossings, disappearance, and noise. The fixed sensory control wins each family. The next candidate should add local state persistence and predictive feedback so the learned code can use recent history when event evidence is missing or corrupted, then check for regressions on clean translation. This remains an experiment until a concrete production integration is reviewed and approved.

The [closed-loop rollout diagnostic](../experiments/2026-09-24-visual-rollout-findings.md) showed useful four-frame learned predictions on clean translation but substantial drift through crossings and rate changes; the fixed sensory forecaster remained ahead. A persistent local latent state must improve these interaction/missing-evidence cases while retaining the current one-step gains over random and shuffled-credit controls.

The [ungated predictive-feedback test](../experiments/2026-09-24-predictive-feedback-findings.md) made latent activity persist through blank input but degraded every forecast family and quadrupled quiet-target false alarms. Do not reuse the emitted event prediction directly as latent input. Next test a **separate locally persistent state transition and observation-emission readout**, both updated by local delayed error, so hidden continuity need not imply a visible event. Keep the successful no-feedback learned code and fixed primitive as matched baselines.

The [first separated-state test and frozen ablation](../experiments/2026-09-24-separated-visual-state-findings.md) sustained latent state and reduced quiet false alarms, but shared event error across observed and imagined emission banks underfit even training scenes. Removing imagined emission worsened clean prediction, so the mechanism needs a different local credit assignment rather than a simple output cutoff. Test one bounded locally separated-credit variant, then pivot if it does not improve both training fit and held-out interruption performance.

The [separate-credit follow-up](../experiments/2026-09-24-separated-emission-credit-findings.md) restored much of the local learner's clean forecast quality, confirming that the shared emission error caused credit interference. It did **not** produce a material advantage over a matched no-recurrence emitter on disappearance or noise. Stop tuning this two-bank variant. The next experiment must change the latent transition's learning target or partial-observation state representation so it can learn continuity through missing evidence, while preserving separate local credit and the strong correlation-input learned code as controls.

The [raw-event re-anchoring test](../experiments/2026-09-24-raw-event-reanchoring-findings.md) found a more basic partial-observation failure: the coincidence-only latent cannot see first appearance or reappearance after a blank frame. Appending raw events restored latent activity at those sites, but greatly worsened prediction, even compared with a random augmented dictionary on disappearance and noise. Freeze that augmented code for one capacity check before deciding whether to change its local predictive target or separate first-sighting input from ordinary motion coding. Do not promote the naive raw-channel augmentation.

The [frozen augmented-code capacity check](../experiments/2026-09-24-raw-reanchoring-capacity-findings.md) found both code interference and a local readout gap. The augmented code still supported 0.644 offline F1 on unseen patterns versus 0.320 for a random dictionary, but this is well below the original coincidence-only code's 0.918; its online local readout also fit training episodes poorly. Test a separate raw first-sighting pathway alongside the intact learned motion code, then local state continuity through missing input. Keep this opt-in until it beats appropriate frozen and fixed controls.

The [split sensory-pathway test](../experiments/2026-09-24-split-sensory-pathway-findings.md) preserved the original 0.918 held-out offline capacity and activated at reappearance, but exact reappearance prediction was only 0.016 F1. A matched opposite-motion pair proves that current reappearance input is identical while the next event differs. The next experiment therefore needs **hidden temporal context**, with a local learned transition and a separate event emission mechanism. Require a state-reset ablation and quiet-frame false-alarm check; sensory separation alone is not enough.

The [split-state recurrent test](../experiments/2026-09-24-split-state-recurrence-findings.md) found that the existing locally learned hidden transition carries activity through blank input yet provides **no causal reappearance benefit** over no recurrence or state reset. It also regresses on clean and robust online prediction. Stop tuning this transition. Test a different local temporal-context representation and credit target that can bind pre-gap motion to reappearance, using the matched opposite-motion pair and reset ablation as hard gates before expanding evaluation. Keep the learned representation generic; do not insert direction channels, object labels, or Pong logic.

The [local context-trace test](../experiments/2026-09-24-local-context-trace-findings.md) supplied a decaying trace of learned motion activity to a separately learned raw-onset pathway. Its context input differed strongly between matched opposite-motion histories, but its forecasts did not, and its exact reappearance improvement over trace reset was only 0.010 F1. Clean prediction stayed intact. Before redesigning plasticity, run one targeted frozen-code capacity check on interruption examples held out by shape; only then decide whether the context code or local credit is the main blocker. Do not tune this trace variant through a parameter sweep.

The [targeted frozen-code capacity check](../experiments/2026-09-24-context-trace-capacity-findings.md) found that the concatenated raw+trace dictionary selected identical latent sources for matched opposite histories and did not outperform a zero-trace dictionary on unseen-shape reappearance. Thus history is lost **at competitive coding**, before predictive credit. Test one different opt-in temporal representation in which raw onset gates locally learned history-selective units; require distinct latent selections for the matched pair before training another predictor. Keep this as an experiment until effective online forecasting transfers.

The [history-gated code test](../experiments/2026-09-24-history-gated-code-findings.md) passed that representation gate: opposite histories with identical current input selected different units at all 13 matched sites, and a frozen post-hoc direction probe transferred on 47/48 unseen-shape cases versus 36/48 for a random dictionary. This is the first causal temporal-context code to survive both onset and shape transfer. Train a separate local delayed-event readout from it next; the code alone is not a forecast or a working M1A architecture.

The [history-gated prediction and broader transfer test](../experiments/2026-09-24-history-gated-prediction-findings.md) showed a real but narrow forecast gain on eight rightward disappearance cases (0.138 exact F1 versus 0.015 random and zero reset). Across 144 unseen-shape, multi-position interruptions, the gain collapsed to 0.027 F1 and **no true positives for up, down, or left**. Do not promote this readout. The code's post-hoc 47/48 direction probe appears insufficient for an additive per-source emission bank; determine whether a local spatial population pattern carries the predictive distinction, then test a local population-context readout or credit rule rather than tuning the same scalar weights.

The [history-code locality probe](../experiments/2026-09-24-history-code-locality-findings.md) rejected the population-only hypothesis: individual learned unit identities at reappearance sites made direction readable on 599/608 held-out sites (98.5%), with no gain from 5×5 or 9×9 aggregation. The current per-source local emission rule still fails three directions. Test one revised local credit normalization using per-unit eligible error instead of a frame-global source denominator; keep the code, event target, and full 144-case transfer gate fixed. If that fails, revisit the objective itself rather than adding population decoding machinery.

The [per-unit credit test](../experiments/2026-09-24-unit-credit-prediction-findings.md) failed: the learned code produced exactly the same 30 true positives and 33 false positives as the old rule on 144 interruptions, all true positives from rightward motion. A random-code version fired in more directions but generated 666 false positives on event frames. Stop denominator and threshold tweaks. Keep the locally legible history code fixed and test a genuinely different local event-learning objective/credit assignment across temporal and ON/OFF/quiet contexts, with all-direction reappearance transfer and false alarms as gates. No production architecture has been approved or changed.

The [frozen future-event capacity diagnostic](../experiments/2026-09-25-history-event-capacity-findings.md) found the decisive local context: history-unit identity alone could not fit future ON/OFF events, but crossing that learned unit with **currently observed raw polarity** yielded 0.923 offline F1 on 144 held-out interruptions across all directions, versus 0.544 for a random code and zero after trace reset. The previous emitter lacked this local sign conditioning. Test a separately indexed ON/OFF local emission bank with the same online delayed-error rule; do not import offline readout weights. Promote only after online transfer and safety/control gates, with explicit user approval for the production revision.

The [online polarity-conditioned readout](../experiments/2026-09-25-polarity-local-prediction-findings.md) made a substantial local-learning gain: 0.289 exact F1 on 144 unseen interruptions versus 0.027 unsplit, 0.088 random, and zero reset, with ordinary prediction and quiet false alarms preserved. It still missed the 0.40 gate and barely predicted down motion; training interruptions reached only 0.488 versus a 0.959 frozen-code ceiling. Test one balanced generic interruption exposure budget with all controls and parameters fixed. No production architecture has changed or been approved.

The [balanced-exposure follow-up](../experiments/2026-09-25-balanced-interruption-exposure-findings.md) raised exact online held-out interruption F1 to **0.434** across all four directions (down 0.464), beating unsplit, random-code, and history-reset controls while preserving clean forecasts and quiet false alarms. It passes the registered transition-level transfer gate. Whole-sequence occlusion improves only slightly over the archived predictor, however, so do not equate this with a working generic world model. Next freeze the learned weights and vary gap length/timing, then test multi-frame continuity and efficient execution before drafting any production architecture revision for approval.

The [frozen timing-transfer test](../experiments/2026-09-25-gap-timing-transfer-findings.md) held out two- and four-frame gaps and found exact reappearance F1 of **0.427** and **0.380** versus zero for history reset and a fixed raw-correlation control. The learned history signal therefore generalizes beyond the trained three-frame gap. The fixed raw sensory control remains much stronger on simple visible motion (0.994 pre-gap; 0.663 just after reappearance), while the learned history forecast is uniquely useful at reappearance. Test their complementary contribution on full scenes using frozen weights and false-alarm controls; do not promote a gap-only forecast as the final visual representation.

The [full-scene complement test](../experiments/2026-09-25-sensory-history-complement-findings.md) found that naive pixelwise fusion adds correct post-gap events but nearly cancels them with false positives: three-frame occlusion F1 was 0.717 versus 0.716 for the fixed raw control, missing the registered +0.05 gate. The learned code remains causally useful at exact reappearance, yet it is not a reliable full-scene forecaster. Before another production proposal, determine whether local source/trace/sensory state can separate beneficial history emissions from false alarms; choose a locally learned reliability mechanism only if that information is present. Otherwise change the temporal representation or objective. Do not promote the fixed raw control as the learned representation.

The [gap-exit audit](../experiments/2026-09-25-gap-exit-audit-findings.md) changes that priority: the motion trace survives every blank interval, but the raw-onset gate leaves **zero active readout sources** on the last blank frame, so all 608 reappearance event pixels per timing window are missed. An oracle that removes every learned-only false positive still caps full three-frame occlusion F1 at 0.747, below the 0.766 gate. The next question is whether the existing trace can locally decode the forthcoming event *before* it is observed. If so, test a continuously active locally plastic readout; if not, the latent state needs a learned recurrent transition during missing evidence. A reliability gate alone cannot solve the measured coverage failure.

The [frozen blank-trace capacity test](../experiments/2026-09-25-blank-trace-capacity-findings.md) found that a 17×17 evaluation-only local readout can forecast **unseen three-frame gap exits at 0.516 F1** from trace alone, versus zero trace reset. This establishes useful pre-reappearance information, but its transfer drops to 0.262 for earlier two-frame gaps and 0.091 for later four-frame gaps; the latter also has only 0.809 geometric target coverage. Check one local trace-normalization control to distinguish amplitude decay from missing spatial state before building an online continuously active readout. Production M1A is unchanged.

## Shortest evidence path before promotion

1. **Generic scene suite.** Extend the opt-in event-camera tests to varied shapes, polarities, scales, positions, speeds, two independent movers, crossings, partial occlusion, stationary visual context, trajectory changes, and sensor noise. The generator may know hidden trajectories only for scoring. Establish performance and throughput of the full proposed front end, not just the isolated kernel.
2. **Latent discovery experiment.** Train a small, opt-in retinotopic recurrent circuit on raw event streams with no motion or object labels. Freeze it and test whether its state transfers across shapes, positions, polarity, and timing. Compare untrained, recurrent-frozen, time-shuffled, and fixed-correlation controls. Report forecast quality *and* whether simple post-hoc probes can read useful visual factors from the latent state; probes never train the circuit.
3. **Grouping and memory experiment.** Test whether interactions among learned latent units preserve separate patterns through crossings and brief occlusion, without ground-truth identity at inference. Score association switches, localization, visibility confidence, and recovery on held-out generated scenes. Compare to the explicit continuity ceiling and instantaneous-event baseline.
4. **Learning experiment.** Use repeated but varied generic visual interactions with visible local context (for example, moving patterns around arbitrary stationary shapes). Compare frozen, fixed extrapolation, and locally learned recurrent forecasts on held-out positions/shapes/interactions. Require a meaningful improvement at multiple horizons, no degradation on ordinary translation, causal updates, and no autograd. A learned circuit should be promoted only if it beats the matched fixed mechanism, unlike the current scalar efficacy.
5. **Out-of-distribution check.** Apply the same inference code unchanged to Pong event streams and at least one non-Pong visual sequence. Evaluate representation and forecast, not game-object extraction. A deterministic, nonlearning controller diagnostic is optional; no reward learning is needed for M1A.

These are experiments first. Promote only a mechanism that passes its gate, with an explicit production diff and tests reviewed before changing the in-place architecture. Current evidence supports further work on generic visual primitives; it does not yet establish coherent tracking or learned world dynamics.
