# Cross-body transfer before hardware

The next question is which knowledge saves interactions when the physical body changes. In the first simulated task a carriage moves a tip on two linear axes. In the second, two revolute joints move a tip along configuration-dependent arcs. The controller sees image-derived tip and target positions. It can request two bounded commands. The body identity, joint angles, channel mapping and kinematics stay in the evaluator. Action-effect estimates update from the consequences of the controller's own commands without backpropagation. The previous Pong checkpoint is the starting point for the research question. Its three discrete paddle actions and game-specific scoring memories are not used as arm weights.

## Comparisons

1. Practice reaching on the carriage or on a nominal arm. Save the acquired model before exposing it to target starts. Compare three instances of the same software on matched target courses: a learner initialized from that source, the identical fresh learner, and source knowledge held fixed. Inspect first-reach interaction costs, success and recovery across repeated reaches on the same body. Include broken action correspondence (remapped channels) and changes in actuator response. Negative transfer matters as much as positive transfer.
2. Learn how to allocate calibration actions across varied simple source bodies. Carry that decision into the arm with its directional estimates reset. Compare against a same-code untrained strategy and an explicit two-axis calibration. Charge probes, misses and timeouts to the same interaction budget as goal pursuit.
3. Learn which visual cue predicts success on a simple body. Keep that reward evidence while forcing the arm to learn its own motors. Flip the rewarded cue without changing the appearance or anatomy to test whether the transferred preference can be corrected. Record actual held reaches and rewards.

The primary motor comparison uses 24 independent streams. Each has 24 source practice reaches and four new target reaches per condition. Each reach has a 96-command horizon. The first reach on a new body measures immediate benefit or interference. The following three measure online recovery. For a useful motor prior, seek a smaller mean time to a successful hold than the matched fresh learner, with no reduction in completed reaches. Report paired uncertainty over independent streams. The acquisition-strategy and reward experiments have separate controls and schedules. Source-only selection and target-exposed development must be identified in reports.

The engineering precedent is adaptive visual servoing through observed image motion. The future SO-101 target raises additional questions about 3D observability, joint limits and calibrated position targets. This iteration uses a planar simulator. The three-stream motor development run used seed 322433. The held-out motor schedule uses seed 1758402197. Its four comparisons are carriage to nominal arm, carriage to rewired arm, nominal arm to the altered actuator response, and nominal arm to the altered and rewired actuator. The arm variants share link lengths. All arms see the same sample of first targets per stream. Intervals use 4,000 paired bootstrap resamples.

The additional position-command test uses 64 new streams with seeds 86400 through 86463. The receiving arm has three serial joints. Its initial posture is drawn using only the Cartesian starting point. The source has two linear actuators. The target motor learner starts empty and knows the number of available commands. Four source trials supply the cue preference. Eight target trials include a hidden reward reversal after the fourth. The comparison counts the first physically verified held target and all steps to success or timeout. It uses only ordinary per-tick scalar score and the public cue image for learning.

The next comparison removes the categorical near/far encoding. A two-choice learner fits five general image-offset features (first- and second-degree coordinates). Independently drawn reward rules favor one of two candidates according to an unknown linear direction or quadratic form. The carriage supplies 24 source reaches. On a three-joint arm, compare a source-trained utility with reset, shuffled source rewards, and a frozen transferred utility. Each arm starts with fresh motor state and receives eight trials before a payoff flip and twelve after. Count first physically rewarded holds and total commands including failed choices. The evaluator can inspect private reward coefficients. The learner gets only the scored consequences of public marker observations.

A separate efficiency comparison will use 64 fresh streams (seeds 27119901–27119964, alternating linear and quadratic rules). Replay source prefixes of 0, 2, 4, 8, 12 and 24 actual carriage reaches, and run one matched receiving-arm trial per prefix. This measures the cost of gaining the visual task knowledge and how much the first receiving-body reach benefits. The source cost includes exploration and corrections. The amount of prior practice is not chosen on the primary arm comparison.
