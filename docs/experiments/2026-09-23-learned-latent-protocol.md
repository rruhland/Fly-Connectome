# Experience-learned local visual latent: first falsification test

This opt-in experiment asks whether visual structure useful for motion can emerge in a small recurrent circuit rather than be specified as fixed direction channels. It is **not** a proposed production implementation. Each event-site latent unit sees a local 5×5 patch of current and preceding ON/OFF events. Initially random, nonnegative sensory synapses change by competitive local Hebbian/Oja updates. Latent-to-latent synapses connect only neighboring retinotopic sites in a 5×5 window and change from local postsynaptic predictive error times presynaptic eligibility after the next observation. No backpropagation, motion/object labels, simulator state, or hand-assigned direction channels enter either update. The same synapses repeat across positions as an experimental local motif; this is not a measured MaleCNS connection claim.

Train online on repeated 3×3-square translations over four directions, two speeds, two contrasts, and two positions. Freeze all synapses. Evaluate next-event prediction and **post-hoc only** nearest-centroid probes of four-way direction from globally pooled sensory latent activity and the combined sensory-plus-recurrent predictive state on held-out plus, L, and bar shapes and positions. The probe's labels must never enter visual-circuit training. Compare learned sensory codes against their random initialization, and learned recurrent prediction against a temporally shuffled recurrent control and the existing fixed local-correlation predictor on the same event targets. Report results by shape, speed, polarity, and runtime. The experiment succeeds only if learned sensory codes transfer materially beyond random codes and recurrent learning provides forecast benefit beyond temporally shuffled credit; otherwise do not promote it.

The test is intentionally narrow. It cannot establish coherent entity identity, long-horizon dynamics, biological plausibility of a specific cell class, or robustness outside simple translations. Subsequent experiments must address those separately.
