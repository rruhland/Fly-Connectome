# Short Tm4 release memory passes the frozen T5 motion gate

The [preregistered 50 ms profile](2026-09-23-tm4-fast-release-protocol.md) changed only the **opt-in Tm4 release baseline timescale** from 250 ms. Tm4/Tm9 neural dynamics, ordinary spikes, measured Tm4→T5 edges and signs/delays, fixed T5 order current, long-term weights, and the pinned checkpoint were unchanged. Blank-only calibration again selected cap 0.10, with added T5 impulse p99 0.005 and zero calibration blank spikes. The [machine-readable result](2026-09-23-tm4-fast-release-results.json) passes every predeclared frozen-feature condition, including the new y=14/speed 1 and y=18/speed 2 controls.

At y=16/speed 2, Tm4 arm support on the comparator's identical future T5d/up gate samples rose **48.7%→93.9%** (+45.2 percentage points). T5c/T5d direction contrasts retained the expected opposite signs and exceeded ten spikes with per-cell majority agreement in all six position/speed cases. On the two new controls, contrasts were **−132/+55** at y=14/speed 1 and **−73/+24** at y=18/speed 2. Across all 12 subtype/case comparisons, preferred-moving/static ratios were **3.03–4.36** and retained at least **99.7%** of their matched frozen-comparator ratios; blank T5 rates remained below 0.001/neuron/tick. The previously failed y=16/speed 1 T5c ratio improved from the 250 ms profile's 3.892 to **4.355**, against a comparator of 4.339.

The faster source-local release therefore resolves the narrow frozen static-specificity failure without sacrificing the large fast-arm gain, on these bar conditions. It has **not** demonstrated online prediction learning, generalized vision, or Pong benefit. Next, a separately versioned opt-in learning experiment should use these measured afferents and causal local traces, keep the physical sensory transmission fixed, and evaluate genuine next-frame anticipation against its own frozen comparator, static controls, and withheld positions/speeds. Production architecture and plasticity remain unchanged until that result is convincing and the user approves an in-place revision.
