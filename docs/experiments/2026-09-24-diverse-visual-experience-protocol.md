# Diverse visual experience at a fixed training budget

Compare two otherwise identical opt-in visual circuits. The square-only arm receives the prior 32 generic event-camera sequences per training pass. The diverse arm receives 32 sequences per pass from square, plus, L, and bar patterns, balanced across the four translation directions and two speeds. Both arms receive three passes (96 episodes) for each sensory dictionary, transition dictionary, and local predictive readout. Vary position and contrast across passes in the diverse arm. Neither model receives shape, direction, speed, position, or contrast labels; these are generator and scoring metadata only. The model, 5×5 local fields, seeds, next-frame target, credit rule, and evaluation thresholds remain fixed.

Test on 48 new diamond, ring, and zigzag sequences at four directions, two speeds, and both contrasts, centered at a position absent from training. These shapes must be disjoint from both training sets. Measure the learned transition code's post-hoc direction probe, the original local predictive-error event and latent F1, and a frozen-code sparse linear future-event decoder fit separately to each arm's own training events. The offline decoder is a capacity diagnostic only and cannot enter the M1A circuit. Include random transition dictionaries and the fixed low-level event-correlation control. Report train and held-out scores, speed and shape breakdowns, and reachable target fraction.

Evidence that varied unlabeled experience improves reusable predictive state requires held-out learned-code offline event F1 at least 0.50 and 0.15 above the square-only arm, without losing the learned-versus-random-code advantage. If the ceiling rises but the local readout remains weak, investigate local credit/readout design. If both remain weak, change temporal representation or context. Record measured differences even if no gate passes. No production architecture promotion follows from this experiment alone.
