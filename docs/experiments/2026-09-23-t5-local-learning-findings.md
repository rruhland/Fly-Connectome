# Physical vertical motion feature does not yet improve local next-frame prediction

The [bounded pilot](2026-09-23-t5-local-learning-protocol.md) ran 40 continuous online training episodes with the frozen T5 vertical-motion current, the existing `FramePrediction` error × causal eligibility equation, and only measured predictive edges entering T5c/d eligible to change. The [result](2026-09-23-t5-local-learning-results.json) compares frozen and trained weights from the same blank-calibrated neural state on training and withheld vertical trajectories. Both comparisons saw identical camera inputs **and identical T5 local feedforward-arrival targets**, so target drift does not explain the result. No non-T5 weight changed; all states and magnitudes stayed finite and within bounds. Full regression: 361 passed, 4 skipped.

The plasticity path was active: **1,050 of 2,315** eligible T5 edges changed, after 151,260 nonzero local update proposals. The local target was eventful in **4.53%** of frame-boundary T5 cell samples. Positive proposals numbered 18,319; negative proposals 132,941. Median absolute edge change was zero and the maximum was 0.00287 after 40 episodes. This is not a no-eligibility failure, but the learned magnitudes moved little and mostly in the shrinking direction.

The preregistered learning gate **failed**. At the withheld y=16 position, one-pixel/frame T5c/T5d event MSE for one direction changed from 0.003932/0.004140 frozen to 0.003933/0.004139 trained; at doubled speed it changed from 0.004566/0.004678 to 0.004566/0.004676. Other direction and training-position comparisons were similarly negligible. Quiet MSE often decreased slightly, but event anticipation did not consistently improve. Zero and one-frame persistence baselines are recorded for each condition in the result. The physical T5 motion preference therefore does not by itself make the present local next-frame objective effective.

The next diagnostic is narrow: partition update proposals by event versus quiet confirmation **on the same measured edge**, quantify opposite-sign cancellation, and compare their contribution to final weights. If rare event-increasing proposals are repeatedly canceled by quiet-decreasing proposals, a bounded event/quiet local plasticity balance is the next opt-in rule experiment. If both contexts point the same way or the event proposals are simply too small, the credit timing or local target must be reconsidered instead. No production learning-rule change follows from this pilot.
