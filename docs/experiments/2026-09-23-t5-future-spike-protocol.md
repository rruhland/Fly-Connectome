# Can T5's local order state anticipate its own future motion spikes?

The passing 50 ms Tm4 supplement produces frozen direction-selective T5c/d spikes, while raw and blank-subtracted physical afferent-arrival targets fail next-frame forecast preflights. Before adding plasticity, test a genuinely local alternative: **future T5 spiking**. On the same frozen circuit and six position/speed cases, at each frame forecast boundary in frames 3–13 read a T5 cell's causal pending order current and its count of spikes over the preceding eight neural ticks. The target is whether that same T5 cell spikes at least once in the next eight ticks. Neither the current order state nor the recent-spike persistence comparator may inspect that future window.

Run the matched up/down moving, static, and bright-blank trials from restored states with all weights fixed. For preferred T5c/up and T5d/down cases, report target prevalence, order-state event/quiet means, order ROC AUC, and recent-spike-count ROC AUC. Report null-direction, static, and blank target prevalence and order AUC where defined. The order current already contributes to T5 membrane drive, so include a specific persistence comparison; a high AUC alone is insufficient evidence of useful forecasting.

The preflight passes only if every one of the six preferred subtype/case combinations has at least ten future spike events, order event mean at least twice quiet mean, order ROC AUC at least 0.70 and at least 0.05 above recent-spike persistence AUC; static future-spike prevalence must be less than half preferred-moving prevalence, and blank future-spike prevalence below 0.001. Passing would justify a separately preregistered **opt-in** local synaptic-credit experiment, not production promotion or a claim that M1A prediction learning works.
