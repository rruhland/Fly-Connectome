# Two-timescale predictive assemblies: opt-in M1A test

**Question:** The learned fast field predicts next-event locations but its active winners do not carry stable transferable motion. Can a separately learned, slower sparse assembly state bind nearby fast motifs across polarity and event gaps, while preserving the fast field's predictive gain?

**Architecture and scope:** Keep the previous signed-event fast motif field and its local predictive-error rule frozen after training. A new shared slow circuit receives a local patch of both current and previous fast activity, not an object crop or direction input. Sixteen assembly types compete on a 4-pixel-stride retinotopic sheet. The winners reconstruct those local fast patterns by local residual updates. A 3×3 recurrent assembly kernel learns to predict the next active assembly from local pre/post activity and postsynaptic error; recurrent support affects later competition. Assembly activity decays through quiet camera frames instead of resetting with each event. The slow circuit can decode its predicted assembly field through its learned sensory motifs and the frozen fast motif dictionary to forecast the next signed event. All updates are local; no backpropagation, labels, object slots, or Pong-specific features enter learning or inference. This stays in `scripts/` and `tests/`; production M1A and measured graph remain unchanged.

**One-shot controls:** Train all sensory motifs on the same 64 unlabeled generic episodes. Compare aligned local recurrent credit, frozen self-persistence recurrence, and time-shuffled recurrent credit with the same sensory motifs and fast input. Also retain the previous fast-only field as the prediction and representation baseline. Do not tune the spatial stride, number of assemblies, decay, learning rates, or score weights on held-out cases.

**Decisive evaluation:** Use the previously registered full-frame position, doubled-speed, changed-shape, separated, and crossing cases, plus 16 held-out generic scenes. No target regions or object counts enter inference. Score next-meaningful-event top-8/top-32 recall from the slow forecast, with the fast forecast as a paired baseline. After inference, an offline frozen direction probe may pool assembly activity in synthetic object neighborhoods; report that as an oracle-localized capacity check, not autonomous tracking. Also count active assembly sites, activity outside those neighborhoods, and duplicate/disconnected active regions relative to the number of synthetic movers *only during evaluation*. A useful M1A foothold requires aligned slow state to beat both slow controls and fast-only on doubled-speed and separated-mover direction transfer (at least 80% each), without materially losing the fast field's held-out next-event forecast or collapsing at crossings. If it fails, stop this assembly variant and reconsider the learning objective/state boundary rather than sweep its parameters.

**Order:** Test local sensory learning, recurrence, temporal persistence, and translation sharing; implement the opt-in circuit and verify focused tests; run the registered comparison once; document the decision and run the full repository suite before committing and pushing the checkpoint.
