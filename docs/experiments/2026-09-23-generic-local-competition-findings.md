# Local competition result and remaining learning gap

The local 7×7 displacement selector repaired the original cross-tempo credit conflict. After 96 unlabeled training episodes, its held-out next-frame event F1 was **0.977** on every shape × speed aggregate, including square, plus, L, and bar at one and two pixels/frame. Separate movers with the same or different speeds also reached 0.977; static interior frames had no repeated forecasts. The prior learner without competition scored 0.699 overall and 0.000 on every one-pixel/frame group. This is evidence for a reusable **local motion primitive** under clean translation, not yet for coherent object understanding.

The matched fixed competitive control also scored **0.977 F1** and had lower candidate MSE (0.045 versus 0.134 learned). The learned efficacy ended near 0.696 for every polarity/offset; its reduction from unity reflects online credit at episode termination and worsened calibration on the interior-frame evaluation. Thus the selector, not weight learning, produced the observed forecast gain. We should **not** claim effective M1A dynamics learning from this experiment. The unit-efficacy uncompetitive control scored 0.845 F1.

The next test should challenge the selector with less ideal visual evidence—noise, clutter, occlusion, and trajectory changes—and establish where local translation alone stops being predictive. A later learning test must give the local rule meaningful visual context and show improvement over the same fixed selector, on held-out scenes. No production M1A architecture was changed.
