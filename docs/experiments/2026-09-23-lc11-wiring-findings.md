# LC11 is a substantial measured branch omitted from M1

The [read-only audit](2026-09-23-lc11-wiring-audit-protocol.md) scanned the raw MaleCNS contact table in batches and matched LC11 inputs to the pinned M1 body IDs. The [results](2026-09-23-lc11-wiring-results.json) record source and table checksums. No neurons or edges were added to the running graph.

The 75 traced right LC11 cells receive 202,464 measured incoming contacts. Neurons already in the M1 roster provide 176,715 of them (87.3%) and reach **every** right LC11 cell. T2, T2a, and T3 alone provide 22,314, 14,549, and 39,187 contacts respectively (76,050 combined; 37.6% of all LC11 input contacts). Each class reaches all 75 targets; 3,689, 2,258, and 4,246 of its respective edges meet the M1 extraction's three-contact path threshold. All selected right-side T2/T2a/T3 cells have excitatory transmitter-sign assignments. T3 is the largest input class by total contact mass. The left-side comparison also has T2, T2a, and T3 reaching all 68 traced LC11 cells, with 14,603, 10,023, and 30,018 contacts respectively.

This confirms that excluding LC11 removes a strongly measured small-object branch. It aligns with physiological evidence that T2/T3 feed LC11 and that T3 can excite it ([Keleş et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7210872/)). The contact totals alone do **not** show motion selectivity or justify a production roster change: all three classes were silent in the frozen dot/bar spike audit, and T3 rest-current calibration failed to generate a reliable ON-plus-OFF excess. An LC11 simulation driven only by these existing spikes would therefore miss much of the biologically relevant signal.

Before implementing an opt-in LC11 decoder, the next bounded test should measure dot and bar **subthreshold** T2/T2a/T3 responses against matched blanks, including their time course and a second location. If those states carry a reproducible, localized signal, an experimental graded-transmission readout on measured T2/T3→LC11 edges is worth testing. If they do not, the defect lies farther upstream and another downstream neuron would only hide it.
