# Existing T4 input arms do not yield transferable direction readout

The [preregistered read-only test](2026-09-23-t4-compartment-input-protocol.md) replayed measured Mi1/Tm3 excitation and Mi9/Mi4 inhibition into separate causal 5 ms traces for every T4 cell. It used no trained readout, changed neuron dynamics, or prediction learning. Opposing 13-position moving bars had matched events; horizontal and vertical motion were checked at x=18 and x=46, with static-bar and no-event controls. The [compact results](2026-09-23-t4-compartment-inputs.json) retain subtype responses and local cell counts.

The simple divisive signal `E/(1+I9+I4)` had substantial positive evoked response, but its horizontal direction contrast reversed between positions **for every T4 subtype**. For the geometry-relevant T4c/d pair, right-minus-left scores were -11.78/-10.19 at x=18, then +5.34/+4.29 at x=46; the pair did not oppose each other. Static-bar responses were as large or larger at x=46. The vertical T4a/b pair likewise moved together (+2.59/+2.79 at x=18, -2.12/-1.39 at x=46), with direction indices below 0.2.

The fixed coincident-disinhibition score sometimes produced a large ratio between tiny values. For example, vertical T4b had +0.00439 right-minus-left at x=18 but -0.00377 at x=46, while T4a's x=18 contrast was only +0.0000075. Horizontal T4c/d did not show opposite preferences and often responded more to a static bar. Mi4's trace remained zero because its baseline neurons did not spike. Local T4 spike excess was between -1 and +1 per subtype and condition, never a robust evoked response. No score passed the proposed cross-position, opposite-subtype, static-control criteria, so this branch stops **before** implementing a compartment neuron. A richer dendritic model remains possible, but these particular active streams and fixed local operations do not justify it yet.

An overlooked task distinction now matters. The original Milestone 1 design explicitly **deferred LC11** (`docs/plans/2026-09-16-milestone-1-design.md`, section 5.1). The pinned graph contains 822 T2, 939 T2a, and 976 T3 cells but no LC11, although the raw annotations contain 143 LC11 cells. Fly experiments identify T2/T3→LC11 as a small-object pathway that can operate partly independently of T4/T5 motion, and T2/T3 respond to both contrast polarities ([LC11 object responses](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5340600/), [T2/T3 inputs and selectivity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7210872/)). This does **not** prove that the simulated T2/T3 pathway works: the saved frozen dot and bar probes show zero global T2/T2a/T3 spikes under all tested directions, polarities, and both locations, with membrane voltages far below the unit threshold. A bounded opt-in operating-point test of the *existing* T3 pathway is therefore the next smaller question. Adding LC11 to the in-place M1 roster would be a separate scope change requiring user approval.
