# Opt-in LC11 object-pathway transmission test

The pinned network has a reproducible local T2/T2a/T3 voltage signal but zero corresponding spikes. The MaleCNS tables record 76,050 right-side T2/T2a/T3→LC11 contacts. This opt-in experiment asks whether a **bounded local voltage-dependent output** on those existing contacts can make a downstream LC11 readout respond. It does not change the M1 roster, checkpoint, neuron execution, or learning rule.

Replay the frozen initial full-graph network with plasticity off. Build a separate 75-cell LC11 adaptive-LIF bank from traced right LC11 identities and their measured contacts from selected right T2/T2a/T3 sources only. Keep source transmitter signs, `gain × contacts` weights, the graph's one-tick delay, 5 ms synaptic current and 20 ms membrane decay, threshold and refractory/adaptation parameters. This bank is a **partial feedforward motif**, not a reconstruction of all LC11 input or output. It cannot demonstrate native LC11 size tuning.

For a spike-only control, transmit the recorded T2/T2a/T3 presynaptic spikes over these contacts. For the experimental mode, replace those spikes on the *same edges* with `release_t = min(f, f × max(0, voltage_t − rest_voltage)/0.003)` where `rest_voltage` is each presynaptic neuron's frozen 500-tick no-event warm voltage and `f` is one of `0.05, 0.10, 0.25`. The maximum release is thus at most 0.25 spike-equivalents per source per tick. A negative or absent voltage change cannot create excitatory release; the sign of every measured edge remains fixed. The 0.003 voltage scale is fixed from the prior x=18 T2 ON dot diagnostic, not tuned on LC11 response. Record the exact per-tick release and current as well as spikes. Reset both upstream and LC11 state from a shared no-event warm baseline for each trial.

Calibrate only on right-moving x=18 ON/OFF radius-1.5 dots versus polarity-matched blanks. Choose the smallest `f` with at least five excess LC11 spikes in the transit window for **each** polarity, fewer than 0.01 blank spikes/neuron/tick, finite voltage/current, and no more than 20% of LC11 cells spiking in any one tick. If none passes, stop. Freeze the first passing value and test x=46, opposite motion direction at both locations, moving bar, and static-dot onset/offset controls. Report raw spikes, per-cell response counts, current modulation, peak spike fraction, and pixel-event counts. A spike response alone is insufficient to claim small-object or motion selectivity; compare bar/static conditions explicitly. No training follows unless the response is reproducible and not dominated by quiet-frame activity.
