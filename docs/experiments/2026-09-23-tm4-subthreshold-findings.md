# Tm4 has latent current at the fast holdout, but static responses are a serious control

The [frozen source-state protocol](2026-09-23-tm4-subthreshold-protocol.md) inspected only measured Tm4→T5 afferents in the withheld y=16, two-pixel/frame condition. It paired each Tm4 source-current sample with a bright-blank trial at the same cell and simulation tick; the graph, signs, delays, weights, and T5 gate were unchanged. The [result](2026-09-23-tm4-subthreshold-results.json) passes its preregistered latent-current screen for the previously unsupported **T5d/up** case.

Of 309 future T5d gate events on that up trial, **164** lacked the Tm4 arm-history support used by the prior audit. Even there, **62.8%** had a positive contact-weighted Tm4 source-current deviation above 0.01; mean positive deviation was **0.0996**, versus **0.0379** on quiet samples (**2.63×**). Only **3 of 207** local T5d cells lacked measured Tm4 contacts. T5c/up showed a related, weaker pattern (52.2% of 249 missing-arm events above 0.01; mean positive deviation 0.0651 versus 0.0159 quiet). Thus absence of a spike-driven Tm4 arrival does not generally mean absence of local Tm4 synaptic state. This is a plausible physical source for an opt-in graded-release test, not evidence that release would improve prediction.

The static-bar control is an important warning: all **23** T5d static-gate events lacking Tm4 arm support also had positive source-current deviation above 0.01, with mean **0.1748**, larger than in the moving missing-arm case. A simple graded output could strengthen static false alarms as readily as useful motion. Any fixed-profile transmission experiment must therefore require improved fast-motion arm coverage **and** preserve moving-versus-static T5 spike selectivity and blank silence across both calibration positions and withheld speeds. Do not promote a Tm4 release mode or run local learning on it without those controls; production architecture and plasticity remain unchanged.
