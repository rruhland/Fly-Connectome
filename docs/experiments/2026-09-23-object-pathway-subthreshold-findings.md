# The object pathway carries local voltage signals that its spikes discard

The [preregistered frozen test](2026-09-23-object-pathway-subthreshold-protocol.md) compared every tick of moving-dot, moving-bar, and static-dot responses with polarity-matched blanks restored from the same 500-tick warm state. It changed no network parameter. The [readable results](2026-09-23-object-pathway-subthreshold-results.json) and [compressed 144-tick aggregate traces](2026-09-23-object-pathway-subthreshold-traces.npz) include source and graph checksums. All 822 T2 and 976 T3 cells, and 938/939 T2a cells, received an approximate readout column from their measured afferents. This column inference did not feed the model.

The preset screen **passed through T2** for ON and OFF dots at both x=18 and x=46. Mean absolute local voltage differences from blank were 0.00314/0.00622 at x=18 (ON/OFF) and 0.00524/0.00732 at x=46, versus opposite-location differences at most 0.00009. The number of local T2 cells with a time-averaged absolute difference above 0.002 was 20/31 at x=18 and 30/49 at x=46, exceeding the required five. T3 also carried an OFF difference of 0.00266/0.00250 at the two locations; its x=18 ON mean was 0.00111, below the screen. The changes occurred in incoming synaptic current as well as membrane voltage. **No local T2/T2a/T3 spikes occurred** in any of these dot trials or matched blanks.

This is a specific representation bottleneck: the current uniform spike threshold suppresses a reproducible, retinotopically local signal before it can reach the measured LC11 pathway. It does not prove that graded output from these cells is the biological implementation, but it justifies a bounded opt-in test of that possibility. The moving bar evoked **larger** voltage changes than the dot for all three classes at both sites, and OFF static-dot responses were sometimes near moving-dot responses. Thus the present upstream state has position and event information, while small-object selectivity, motion specificity, and prediction remain unproven. Those controls must stay in any LC11 experiment. The direction of motion was fixed here; a transferable motion code was not tested.

The next experiment can add the 75 recorded right LC11 cells and **only their measured edges** to an isolated graph copy, retaining original signs/contact weights/delays and the pinned baseline. Compare spike-only transmission with a bounded voltage-dependent release on existing T2/T2a/T3→LC11 edges, calibrated on blanks without response-label tuning. Demand a repeatable local dot response at two locations and both polarities, with bar/static controls, before any learning-rule experiment or production architecture proposal. The primary fly study found T2/T3 responses to both contrast polarities and functional excitation of LC11 by T3, supporting this direction without specifying a graded-release law ([Keleş et al., 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7210872/)).
