# Local event motion survives shape and multi-object controls

The [generic motion-map protocol](2026-09-23-generic-motion-map-protocol.md) kept an event-local signed x/y field instead of pooling the entire image. It used no shape, trajectory, direction, or speed label at inference, no fitted parameters, and no Pong-specific region. In the [frozen result](2026-09-23-generic-motion-map-results.json), it decoded **128/128** single-object directions across four unchanged shapes, four directions, two speeds, two positions, and both contrast polarities. The largest wrong-axis/primary-axis score ratio was **0.133**. Static onset/offset controls had zero motion energy during the scored interior frames.

In two separated multi-object scenes, local regions gave the correct **opposite** direction signs for both movers while the static distractor had no direction. In a temporary-overlap scene, both signs were correct before and after the merged frames; identity during the fully merged interval was not evaluated. The nearly 100% energy-localization number on single-object uniform backgrounds is expected and should not be treated as evidence of general scene segmentation.

This validates a reusable *fixed local motion primitive* on controlled binary scenes. It does **not** show that entities are discovered or tracked without evaluator masks, that temporal dynamics are learned, that an SNN can express the same computation through measured fly anatomy, or that the primitive handles texture, camera motion, or complex occlusion. The next decisive opt-in test is a causal local transition learner trained on unlabeled event sequences and evaluated on held-out shapes and positions. It should predict future local events better than persistence without direction or object labels.
