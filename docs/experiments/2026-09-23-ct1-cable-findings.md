# CT1 node-level cable assignment stops at its attachment check

The [predeclared skeleton protocol](2026-09-23-ct1-cable-protocol.md) downloaded the official MaleCNS SWC for CT1_L body 10009 and attempted to attach the already deduplicated 32,728 Tm1/Tm9 input and 37,186 T5c/d output sites. The 43,761-node skeleton and synapse sites occupy matching MaleCNS coordinate ranges, but the [result](2026-09-23-ct1-cable-results.json) fails the required nearest-node attachment screen: **median 140.4 voxels (1.12 µm)** for input sites versus the prespecified 125-voxel limit. Output median was **119.9 voxels (0.96 µm)**; input/output 95th percentiles were **238.0/211.6 voxels**. The script stopped before any shortest-path or cable-nearest source assignment, so it makes **no claim** about CT1 branch identity or a cable-based motion rescue.

Projecting each site to its nearest SWC **segment** rather than nearest node could reduce sampling error, but that would be a new geometry method requiring its own quality gate. After the failed same-column and Euclidean-nearest shadows, another CT1 approximation would not yet address the central M1A learning bottleneck. Keep the measured CT1 omission and real spatial offset as findings, without promoting either shadow or changing production topology. Resume the broader motion-stage objective: identify where useful motion information is expressed and test a local credit rule against that signal before proposing an in-place architecture revision.
