# Measured inhibitory LC11 contacts are functionally silent in the current spiking model

The [bounded signed-input test](2026-09-23-lc11-signed-input-protocol.md) expanded the opt-in 75-cell LC11 bank to **all** 38,172 contacts from pinned M1 source neurons. It retained raw contact weights, the sources' fixed transmitter signs, and one-tick delay. The [results](2026-09-23-lc11-signed-input-results.json) record source-class spike activity, checksums, and the ignored per-tick trace artifact. No production graph or learning rule changed.

Those inputs contain 121,656 excitatory and 55,059 inhibitory contacts. Yet major inhibitory sources, including Li15 (8,175 inhibitory contacts), MeLo10 (7,044), Li26 (4,191), and TmY19a/b (7,714 combined), produced **zero** spikes in both blank and x=18 dot captures. Of the recorded source classes only four fired at all during blank: Tm3 (82 spikes), Tm2 (21), Li17 (1), and Li28 (1); none supplies meaningful measured inhibition to LC11 under these probes. T2/T2a/T3 also remained spike-silent.

Consequently, the full signed-input bank was nearly identical to the T2/T2a/T3-only bank. At release fractions 0.05/0.10/0.25, ON excess LC11 spikes were `0/0/-1`, OFF excess `0/+1/+3`, and blank rates `0.00069/0.01097/0.04181` spikes/neuron/tick. No setting passed the preset both-polarities criterion; the x=46, opposite-direction, bar, and static follow-ups were skipped. The spike-only bank had zero LC11 spikes. At `f=0.05`, mean absolute ON dot current modulation was 0.003079 versus 0.003075 in the excitatory-only adaptive bank: the extra measured inputs barely influenced physical current. This is a failure of the simulated **transmission state**, not a lack of anatomical inhibitory edges.

The next question is narrowly testable without another release-law sweep: do the major LC11 inhibitory source cells carry event- or context-dependent *subthreshold* membrane signals? If yes, a hybrid graded E/I release model or local compartment interaction becomes an evidence-based opt-in architecture experiment. If no, the model needs upstream visual dynamics or transduction repair before LC11 can be useful. This directly addresses why the current point-spiking network cannot reproduce LC11 small-object selectivity and why another visual prediction rule would lack a viable target.
