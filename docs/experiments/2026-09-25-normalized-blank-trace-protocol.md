# One local normalization control for blank-trace timing transfer

The frozen last-blank-frame motion trace supports 0.516 future-event F1 at the trained three-frame gap, but only 0.262 on an early two-frame gap and 0.091 on a late four-frame gap. The latter also has only 0.809 target reach within the 17×17 local field. This evaluation-only control separates loss of trace **amplitude** from loss of spatial reach or relative unit pattern.

At each retinotopic site, divide the 24 trace-channel activities by their sum when that sum is nonzero. Leave all-zero sites zero. This is a local divisive normalization: it preserves active sites and relative channel activity, changes no graph connection or learned code weight, and uses no trajectory label. Fit the same ridge-damped, translation-shared 17×17 offline linear readout on the same 64 training-shape three-frame cases. Score the same 144 unseen three-frame cases and 48 cases each for early two-frame and late four-frame gaps at the same 0.5 threshold. Compare directly with the unnormalized frozen-capacity result and confirm geometric reach is unchanged. No offline decoder weight enters online training or production.

Treat normalized trace as a plausible input to a future online local readout only if unseen familiar-gap F1 remains at least 0.45, early-gap F1 reaches at least 0.35, and late-gap F1 reaches at least 0.20. These are capacity criteria, not proof that local online credit or quiet-frame control will work. If they fail, stop tuning trace scale and test a learned recurrent transition that moves latent state through blank frames. This experiment makes no production architecture change.
