# Existing T5 inhibition does not supply transferable null suppression

The [predeclared frozen timing audit](2026-09-23-motion-inhibition-timing-protocol.md) replayed up/down vertical bars, static bars, and blank on the unchanged opt-in Tm9-release/T5-order circuit. The [per-tick result](2026-09-23-motion-inhibition-timing-results.json) records signed predictive stores, actual feedforward current and arrivals, T5 local current, spikes, and predictive-arrival phases on anatomy-local T5c/d groups plus LPi34/43 and VS. Opposite directions had equal event counts; all cases started from the same blank-calibrated state. The source checkpoint and long-term weights were unchanged.

T5c's locally selected cells had **zero inhibitory predictive arrivals and zero inhibitory current** in both moving directions at all four position/speed conditions, although they spiked 153–242 times per direction. T5d had inhibition at y=10 and withheld y=16 but only during **downward**, its *preferred*, speed-one motion (mean magnitude 0.00130 and 0.00080 per cell/tick); y=22 had none. At withheld speed two, the two directions produced nearly identical small inhibition (0.001018 down, 0.000997 up). Thus neither subtype met even the preregistered null-greater bias prerequisite, before asking whether any inhibition arrives early enough to suppress null responses. At y=10, T5d's inhibitory arrivals fell at within-frame ticks 2 and 5 while preferred-direction spikes occurred at all eight tick phases; a simple claim that this inhibition suppresses the initial response would be unsupported.

LPi34 and LPi43 inherited some inhibitory arrivals at y=10 and y=16/speed one, but none at y=22. LPi43's largest mean inhibition (0.0412 per cell/tick at y=10) occurred for its inherited **preferred** downward direction, not the null direction. At withheld speed two, both LPi pools had nearly equal up/down inhibition. Neither LPi pool spiked in any probe. VS received more regular inhibition, but both directions and the static control drove it, and it lacked a preassigned vertical preferred sign. These responses do not justify an inhibitory learning objective on the current downstream pools.

This is a limitation of the **current extracted graph and frozen experimental dynamics**, not evidence that the biological T5 circuit lacks null-direction inhibition. The [roster audit](2026-09-23-ct1-roster-audit-findings.md) showed that the selected right-side T5 receives 80,978 measured contacts from left-soma CT1 body 10009, which the soma-side extraction omits. The experimental physiology identifies a local Tm1/Tm9→CT1→T5 sign-inverting route for null-direction suppression ([Braun et al., Current Biology 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2023.05.007)). The available local `connectome-weights` file is body-to-body only, while the official MaleCNS release provides a separate synapse-partner file with per-contact coordinates ([MaleCNS download](https://male-cns.janelia.org/download/)). The next bounded step is to audit whether the measured partner columns on both sides of CT1 admit a defensible local-compartment approximation. A point-neuron CT1 insertion would contradict the known local electrical isolation and is not a valid rescue test.
